## 1. What each specialty does on its own, its range, how often, and whether it stacks

### Takeaway
Only a few specialties act on the world by themselves around a Pokémon's home. These are Litter (drops materials), Gather (moves items into a community box and on into machines), the processing specialties Chop, Burn, Crush and Recycle (they work from a nearby community box or furnace), Grow, Water (needs a filled water basin) and Generate (temporary power). All the others act only when you talk to the Pokémon, lead it somewhere, or bring it an item. Exact tile ranges are published only for Litter (where items land), Gather (where the box must be) and some devices. No source gives a tile range for Burn, Grow, Water or Generate. Grow does not stack, Litter stacks per Pokémon, and Gather needs only 1–2 Pokémon per site.

### Cited Findings
**Automatic (acts near its home without being asked)**
- [Official] Litter: "Pokémon with the Litter specialty will drop useful materials near their homes". Serebii adds: "As time passes, each Pokémon will bring an item… The higher the Pokémon comfort level, the more items that get brought". — [Serebii Litter](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/litter.shtml)
- [Community-tested] Litter location depends on the home (すみか), not on where the Pokémon is. Kit house: items land on the 1 tile around the house, diagonals not included. Custom-built house: items land on the floor inside and on the 1 tile outside the walls. — [kyu49 ちらかすの仕様](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/)
- [Community-tested] Litter needs a block as ground on the target tile; doors and wire mesh do not count as ground. Nothing lands on a tile that holds an object, except an open door. Open あげさげまど (sash windows) do not get items, but an open フタつきまど (lidded window) does. Nothing lands on a tile with liquid. If few tiles qualify, drops pile onto those tiles. Gather Pokémon pick the items up. A Litter Pokémon does not drop while it is in Ditto's view. — [kyu49 ちらかす](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/); [kyu49 回路に有用なアイテム](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/circuit/items/)
- [Unverified] kyu49 itself marks these as unconfirmed: a drop check every 15 minutes; no new drops once 5 or more items are lying uncollected; honey capped at 1. — [kyu49 ちらかす](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/)
- [Community-tested] Each drop is a random amount: "sometimes it's one, other times it's two or four". ### Inferences
- The planner's specialty blurbs need fixing: "Collect: Collects coins" (it trades relics and feathers), "Gather Honey: Collects honey" (it trades honey for patterned furniture), "Hype: Boosts nearby Pokémon" (it raises town mood when music plays), and "Fly: Carries you to places" (only to the Pokémon being searched for). The planner's "Gather" does match しわける.
- For work-range overlays, the only specialty with a published tile box is Gather: 4 tiles out from the habitat area, +10/−8 vertically. For Burn, Grow, Water and Generate the safest overlay is the home's own white-dotted habitat or comfort area, marked as an estimate.
- Litter overlays can be exact for houses: the orthogonal ring 1 tile around a kit house, and floor plus 1 tile outside the walls for a built house. The planner can flag ring tiles that hold furniture, liquid, doors or no block as dead tiles.

### Gaps
- No tile range was found for Burn (auto-lighting or furnace pickup), Grow, Water (distance from the basin or from the watering Pokémon), Generate, Hype (distance to the music) or Scrub.
- No source states where a Litter Pokémon drops items when it lives in a habitat rather than a house.
- No measured Litter interval; the 15 min / 5 item cap / honey cap of 1 is unconfirmed. The per-drop amounts conflict (1, 2 or 4 versus 3–6).
- The wastepaper → paper ratio (the planner assumes 2 paper per wastepaper) and the Tinkagear ratio (one summary says 1 ingot → 3) were not seen in readable text.

## 2. Machines and items that need a specialty or an input

### Takeaway
The smelting furnace (ようこうろ) takes one kind of material at a time and needs a Burn Pokémon close by. It converts ores 1:1 and also makes glass and Pokémetal. The concrete mixer needs a Crush Pokémon, limestone and water; guides say its hose must reach water. Paint comes from Crush and berries. The furnace-kit generator burns fuel, the waterwheel needs running water and the sprinkler needs a water source within its diamond. Lights such as campfires and candles are lit by a Burn Pokémon. Rates are known only as batch sizes, with one rough timing for lumber (about 1 minute) and one for concrete (about 1 hour).

### Cited Findings

- [Community-tested] Ore to ingot is 1:1 for copper, iron and gold. — [Game8 JP どう](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768289); [Game8 JP てつ](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768291); [Game8 JP きん](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768292)

- [Official] Generators: Mini generator 5 units, automatic. Windmill 10 units, or 20 at high altitude, automatic. Waterwheel 20 units, automatic with running water under the wheel. Furnace kit 30 units, "Requires Renewing" (you put burnable items in). Each electric item uses 1 unit, except string lights and surface lights, which use 0. Overload shorts the whole circuit. — [Serebii Electricity](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/electricity.shtml); [Game8 EN electricity](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/586144)
- [Community-tested] The furnace-kit generator stops when its fuel runs out. Spare lumber or food are recommended as fuel. — [Game8 JP 電気](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768180). [Unverified] One guide says a Fire-type Pokémon nearby handles the burning. — search summary of [gameranbu](https://gameranbu.jp/pocoapokemon/a04285cc190c9cf2bc46)
- [Official] Power transmission: a utility pole or wireless transmitter reaches 10 blocks from a generator and 15 between transmitters. Distance is Manhattan (one axis, then the other), with at most 5 blocks up or down. Each connects up to 20 things. Kit dens and huts are not powered from outside, but a mini generator inside powers everything in them. Cottages and houses connected to power have everything inside powered. — [Serebii Electricity](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/electricity.shtml); [gamepedia 電気](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4588)
- [Official] Cash register: "Place this on a table and power it on, and Pokémon will come by to conduct trades!" — [Serebii items](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/items.shtml)
- [Official] Relocation and demolition kits: "Put this in front of a building, and Pokémon with the Bulldoze specialty will help you". — [Serebii items](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/items.shtml)
- [Community-tested] Dry rock (さらさらいわ): every 5 seconds it dries 10 tiles chosen from 41, from 1 above to 2 below the rock. Rain or sprinklers stop it, and it restarts when they are gone. It does not restart near a running liquid clock circuit. Crops it kills (except wheat) and berries from trees it dries fall to the ground. — [kyu49 harvest](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/harvest/); [kyu49 water](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/water/); [kyu49 tree](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/tree/)
- [Community-tested] Placement caps: furnaces, metal furnaces (メタルなようこうろ) and concrete mixers together max 128. Workbenches and cooking tools together max 128. Community boxes 32. CD players 32. Generators 64 (raised to 128, furnaces excluded, in Ver. 2.0.0). Poles and wireless transmitters 252 (Serebii says 256). Powered items 511 (raised to 1,024 in Ver. 1.1.0). Farm crops 511 (576 on Cloud Islands). Berry trees 226. — [kyu49 limitation](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/); [Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)

### Inferences
- The planner's process table agrees with the batch sizes for lumber (0.2 log per lumber) and brick (0.5 clay per brick). Its Tinkagear entry (1 ingot each) and Concrete entry (1 limestone each) go against the 1→3 and 1→4 claims, but those claims are unverified, so keep the values and flag them. Glass should list beach sand, sandstone and volcanic ash as alternative inputs.
- A production check can require: a furnace holding one material type, with a Burn home nearby (or a Gather home and community box feeding it); a concrete mixer with a water tile in reach and a Crush home nearby; a sprinkler with water at its own height inside Manhattan radius 5; a waterwheel with flowing water under it; a furnace generator with fuel; a cash register on a table with power.

### Gaps
- No per-item processing time was found for ingots, glass, brick, paint or paper, and no capacity was found for the furnace or mixer. Only the 50-lumber and 50-paint request caps are known.
- The mixer's water needs (how close, still or flowing water) and its yield are not confirmed by any readable source.
- No source gives how much fuel the furnace generator burns per hour, or how long one lumber or one food item lasts.
- No source gives how many waterings one filled water basin provides, or the radius the Water Pokémon covers.

## 3. Litter drops, Gather and Collect pickup, and storage

### Takeaway
Each Litter Pokémon always drops the same material: leaf, vine rope, sturdy stick, honey, small log, nonburnable garbage, stone, twine, fluff, squishy clay, iron ore, stardust, seashell or black paint. Comfort raises the amount. Gather Pokémon put dropped items into a community box in range and can feed machines from it. Collect has nothing to do with picking things up. Crops and berries on the ground cannot be gathered and vanish when you change area. Other dropped items are capped at 512.

### Cited Findings
- [Community-tested] Litter item for each Pokémon. Leaf: Venusaur, Vileplume. Vine rope: Bellsprout, Weepinbell, Tangela, Tangrowth, Snivy, Servine, Serperior. Sturdy stick: Cacturne. Honey: Combee. Small log: Haxorus. Nonburnable garbage: Grimer, Muk, Garbodor, Dhelmise (DLC). Stone: Blissey, Rampardos, Bastiodon, Tyrantrum, Aurorus. Twine: Spinarak, Ariados, Larvesta, Volcarona. Fluff: Mareep, Flaaffy, Swablu, Altaria, Jumpluff. Squishy clay: Paldean Wooper, Clodsire, Trapinch. Iron ore: Glimmet, Glimmora. Stardust: Jirachi (event only). Seashell: Shellder, Cloyster (DLC). Black paint: Seadra, Kingdra (DLC). — [Serebii Litter](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/litter.shtml); [Game8 EN Litter](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585452). Game8 JP's and gamepedia's Japanese lists match this set. — [Game8 JP 自動化](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769942); [gamepedia 自動回収](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3123)
- [Community-tested] Some Litter Pokémon fit in the smallest kit (おへやキット): Spinarak, Wooper, Glimmet, Glimmora, Swablu, Snivy, Combee and Vileplume. Others need at least a hut kit (こやキット): Aurorus, Haxorus, Clodsire, Cacturne, Blissey and Venusaur. — [kyu49 ちらかす](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/)
- [Community-tested] The amount dropped rises with comfort (すみごこち). — [Game8 JP 自動化](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769942); [Serebii Litter](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/litter.shtml)
- [Community-tested] A Litter Pokémon does not drop items while Ditto is watching it, so collection sites should be built a little away from where you usually are. — [Game8 JP 自動化](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769942); [gamepedia 自動回収](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3123)
- [Community-tested] At most 25 Pokémon show in one area. Extra Pokémon are not displayed and may stop giving materials. — [gamepedia 自動回収](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3123). Pokémon in kit houses go inside to sleep, and those indoors do not count toward the 25. — [kyu49 ちらかす](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/)
- [Community-tested] The collection device cannot get copper, wastepaper or sea glass; Dream Islands are the suggested source for those. — [gamepedia 自動回収](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3123)
- [Community-tested] Moving items out of the community box to storage is manual. Tip: use one box per material and pre-fill every slot with 1 of that item, so new drops stack without mixing. — [gamepedia 自動回収](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3123)
- [Official] Ver. 2.0.0: crafting uses materials in a storage box next to the workbench before those in the bag. It also added bulk moves between the storage box and bag. — [Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)
- [Community-tested] Dropped-item cap: 512. Beyond that the oldest drops vanish. Fallen crops and berries vanish when you change area; broken-block drops do not. Valuables that fall out of reach reappear in front of the Pokémon Center. — [kyu49 limitation](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)
- [Community-tested] Storage caps: storage furniture 256 in total; big storage box 256 (counted separately); baskets 32; plates 127; Gulpin and Swalot counted on their own. — [kyu49 limitation](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)
- [Community-tested] Berry trees grow 1 berry per hour, up to 3. Dried trees do not grow berries. Berries fall on one of 6 fixed spots set by the tree's original facing. — [kyu49 tree](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/tree/)

### Inferences
- For a planner check, a working Litter → Gather site is: a Litter home whose drop tiles are valid, plus a Gather home whose white-dotted area, extended by 4 tiles across and +10/−8 vertically, wholly contains one community box. Processing needs a matching Chop, Burn, Crush or Recycle home near that box, or a furnace or mixer near the Gather home.
- Paint for Kingdra and Seadra (black paint) and seashells for Shellder and Cloyster are DLC-only litter, so the planner should tag them as needing the Expansion Pass.

### Gaps
- No source says whether Gather picks up items dropped by the player or broken-block drops, beyond "items on the ground".
- No source gives how fast a Gather Pokémon moves items, or how many it carries per trip.

## 4. How comfort, habitat and home affect work

### Takeaway
The only documented effect of comfort on work is that Litter output rises with comfort. No source says an unhappy Pokémon stops working, and none says a favourite item boosts processing speed. Placement relative to the home matters a lot: processing and gathering are tied to boxes and furnaces near the Pokémon's habitat.

### Cited Findings
- [Official] "The higher the Pokémon comfort level, the more items that get brought" (Litter). — [Serebii Litter](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/litter.shtml)
- [Official] A Pokémon without a habitat or home has comfort 0. Comfort rises when a request is met, and with daily gifts and talks. It rises with a preferred environment (warm, humid, dark…) and falls if disliked things are near the habitat. A built house raises comfort faster than a bare habitat. There are five levels: Iffy, Average, Nice, Great, Awesome. — [Serebii Environment Level](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/environmentlevel.shtml)
- [Community-tested] Comfort factors, roughly in order of importance: no disliked environment in range; liked environment met; not hungry; rest, toy and decoration items all in range; some favourite items in range (duplicates add little); kit house > house > habitat (a hypothesis); a road connecting to the Pokémon Center. The recognition range is −4 to +3 tiles from the door. — [kyu49 家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)
- [Official] Ver. 2.0.0: Pokémon whose habitat is destroyed move back if the same habitat is rebuilt nearby. Pokémon upset by removed furniture notice its return sooner. — [Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)
- [Community-tested] Simple homes work for automation: a block-and-door house with 3 furniture items is enough. One such house holds 3–4 Pokémon. — [Game8 JP 自動化](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769942); [gamepedia 自動回収](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3123)
- [Community-tested] Feeding a Crush Pokémon its favourite food before asking for paint was tested by gamepedia to see whether it raises the rare white and black paint chance. The result could not be read in the page excerpt. — [gamepedia えのぐ](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/2988)

### Inferences
- The planner can show expected Litter output as a function of comfort tier only qualitatively (higher is better), and should not block a setup because of low comfort.

### Gaps
- No source says whether Iffy-comfort or hungry Pokémon refuse requests or stop processing.
- No numbers link comfort tier to Litter amount.
- No evidence was found that favourite items speed up Chop, Burn or Crush processing.

## 5. Community setups, measured rates, quirks and update changes

### Takeaway
The best-documented setups are: (a) Litter + Gather + community box, with processing Pokémon or a furnace nearby; (b) kyu49's liquid and door clock circuits, which collect Litter drops without a Gather Pokémon; (c) sprinkler-centred farms with one Grow Pokémon and a Water Pokémon or a water source, where harvest stays manual. Measured rates are scarce: dry rock (every 5 s, 10 of 41 tiles), berries (1 per hour, max 3) and lumber (about 1 minute per log, unverified).

### Cited Findings
- [Community-tested] Game8 recipe: 1–2 Gather, 3 or more Litter, homes grouped together, community box nearby. A processing Pokémon nearby does the processing, for example clay → brick with Burn or garbage → iron with Recycle. A furnace nearby plus an iron-dropping Litter Pokémon gives iron ingots. — [Game8 JP 自動化](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769942)
- [Community-tested] Self-processing pairs: Haxorus (Litter: small log; Chop → lumber) and Garbodor (Litter: garbage; Recycle → iron ore). Blissey (Litter: stone, plus Trade). — [Game8 EN auto farm](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/586345); [Game8 EN Recycle](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585690)
- [Community-tested] One Pokémon per material covers every Litter item: Venusaur, Bellsprout, Cacturne, Combee, Haxorus, Grimer, Spinarak, Mareep, Wooper, Bastiodon, Glimmet and Jirachi, plus 1 Gather Pokémon. — [gamepedia 自動回収](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3123)
- [Community-tested] kyu49 Litter automation without Gather. Liquid on a drop tile blocks littering, so water is switched on and off with a clock circuit. An open door counts as a valid drop tile, and a closing door pushes items east; kyu49 uses two doors, one always open, driven by a clock. kyu49 recommends "water-flow method 3" for stability, with kit houses so sleeping Pokémon do not count toward the 25 display limit. Sensors on different houses fall out of step when you change area, so they need to switch at exactly the same time (for example via wireless transmitters). — [kyu49 ちらかす](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/)
- [Community-tested] A floor switch triggers everything in a 5×5×5 cube; a wall switch or card reader triggers a 7×7×7 cube. Lidded windows (フタつきまど) are the preferred clock element; they cycle in 18 units versus 31 for sash windows. — [kyu49 回路に有用なアイテム](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/circuit/items/)
- [Community-tested] Water reaches 25 tiles along a channel (12 at low level). Lava reaches 12 (6 at low level). A waterfall lifts items at most 14 tiles. — [kyu49 water](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/water/); [Serebii Water](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/water.shtml)
- [Community-tested] Farm layout: put a sprinkler at the centre over a 1-tile hole of water capped with a pier (さんばし) so it always has water. Add a Water Pokémon with a basin if you have no sprinkler, and move a Grow Pokémon in. Harvest with Cut; powered-up Cut covers 3×6 tiles. Eight farm plots make the "Fresh vegetable field" habitat (フレッシュ野菜畑). — [Game8 JP 自動化](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769942); [gamepedia 畑](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3026)
- [Community-tested] Semi-automatic harvest: kill crops with dry rock, which drops them (except wheat), then wash them to you with water. Vegetables snag on dead crops, so stepped channels are used. Fully automatic harvest is not possible, because Gather ignores crops and dropped crops vanish when you change area. — [kyu49 harvest](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/harvest/)
- [Community-tested] Rates measured or reported: dry rock dries 10 of 41 tiles every 5 s ([kyu49 harvest](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/harvest/)); berries grow 1 per hour, max 3 ([kyu49 tree](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/tree/)); lumber takes about 1 min per log ([Unverified], search summary). Every guide suggests advancing the Switch clock to skip Litter, processing and growth timers. — [Game8 EN Litter](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585452); [Game8 JP 鉄](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768194)
- [Official] Update changes that touch automation. Ver. 1.1.0 (9 Jun 2026): processing Pokémon prefer the box or furnace nearest their own habitat; fixed Pokémon stopping partway through box processing; powered-item cap raised from 512 to 1,024. Ver. 1.1.1 (19 Jun 2026): fixed areas misbehaving when too many items exceed a limit. Ver. 2.0.0 (5 Aug 2026): added Scrub; Water and Chop gained new uses; fixed paint not being produced correctly from berries in the community box; generator cap raised from 64 to 128 (furnaces excluded); Paint, Gather Honey and Collect repeatable in one conversation; relics appraised 10 at a time; crafting draws from a storage box next to the workbench first; Leafage reverts farm soil; certain roof items now block water; laser sensors and switches can trigger the Tidal Bell and Clear Bell. — [Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)

### Inferences
- A planner production check should treat "Litter + Gather + box in range" as the core loop. It should add processing links only when a matching specialty home, or a furnace or mixer, is within the Gather or processing Pokémon's area. It should warn when a Litter drop ring is fully blocked, holds liquid, or is in front of the player's usual base.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, X or YouTube rate measurements (items per hour per Litter Pokémon, ingots per hour) could be read; those hosts were blocked here.
- Serebii's site menu mentions a second DLC, "Part 2: Dazzling Decorations", but no patch notes or mechanics for it were found up to Nov 2026.
