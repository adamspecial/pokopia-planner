# Kit houses in Pokémon Pokopia (ぽこあポケモン): interiors, floors, height and what counts

Method note: in this session, page fetches to kyu49.github.io, game8.co, serebii.net and bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net were blocked by the network allowlist, so every finding below comes from search-engine snippets and summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the whole page. Quotes are as short as the snippets. Labels: **[Confirmed]** = in-game data or an in-game message reported by a wiki or guide; **[Community-tested]** = a player experiment or a consistent report across several community sources; **[Unverified]** = one anecdote, a guess, or sources that disagree. The Japanese title is ぽこあポケモン (ぽこポケ). Kit size tiers in Japanese: おへや = den, こや = hut, いえ = cottage, りっぱな(いえ) = house (two floors).

## 1. Which kits have their own interior, and which are built from blocks?

### Takeaway
There are two kinds of kit building. "Building with Interior" kits load a separate interior scene, with a fixed furniture grid. Some kits instead place ordinary blocks in the world. Bulbapedia names the **Orange hut** and the **Log cabin** as block-built houses. The planner's group "cottages, log cabin" is only partly backed up: the Orange hut, a hut, is block-built, and I found no source that names cottages in general as block-built.

### Cited Findings
- [Confirmed] Serebii lists the City house kit as a "Building with Interior", with 2 floors, 4 residents, ready the next day, width 8 and depth 6. — [Serebii: City house kit](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/build/cityhousekit.shtml)
- [Confirmed] Bulbapedia, Orange hut kit: "The hut is a building made out of blocks, and therefore cannot be relocated or demolished with relocation or demolition kits." — [Bulbapedia: Orange hut kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Orange_hut_kit)
- [Confirmed] Bulbapedia's Log cabin kit page has the same block-built note: it can't be relocated or demolished. The cabin houses up to 4 Pokémon, costs 20 Leaf, 20 Small Log and 20 Lumber, and needs 7 Pokémon to build (Build, 2 Grow, 4 of any specialty). — [Bulbapedia: Log cabin kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Log_cabin_kit)
- [Unverified] The sources disagree on the Log cabin's capacity. The Games Wiki kit guide gives 2 ("cottage-sized cabin"), while Bulbapedia gives 4. Pokopia Companion gives the cabin's size as 10×10×8 and says it is ready the next day. — [The Games Wiki: Building Kits Guide](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/building-kits-guide); [Pokopia Companion: Log cabin kit](https://www.pokopiacompanion.com/kits/log-cabin-kit)
- [Confirmed] Other kits Bulbapedia calls "made out of blocks and building items" (or nature items) are non-house kits: Abandoned Power Plant, Altar of Flame, Freezing Chambers, Fountain plaza and Relaxing park. — [Bulbapedia: Abandoned Power Plant kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Abandoned_Power_Plant_kit); [Bulbapedia: Relaxing park kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Relaxing_park_kit)
- [Confirmed] The Yellow cottage kit has a loft as a second level. Its interior furniture list reads like world blocks and furniture rather than a fixed interior: "Bridge planks, wooden handrail, towel rack, waterproof seat, bathtub, shower, toilet, modern sink, cooking stove, kitchen table, plain chest, plain lamp, pop art lamp, antique clock, wooden partition, wooden ladder, iron ladder". — [Bulbapedia: Yellow cottage kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/yellow_cottage_kit)
- [Confirmed] The Games Wiki describes "Block House" versions of the Hut and Cottage sizes, where the "interior matches external dimensions; standard block-grid placement". It says custom block houses hold up to 4 Pokémon whatever their size. — [The Games Wiki: Dwellings and Houses](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/dwellings-and-houses); [The Games Wiki: Building](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/building)
- [Community-tested] Moving into and out of a kit house goes through a loading screen. For a self-built (block) house, the space enclosed by walls and a door is what counts as the house. — [kyu49 (ぽこポケ学会): 家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)
- [Confirmed] Pokopia Guide counts 62 construction kits in total (DLC included). — [Pokopia Guide: All 62 Construction Kits](https://pokopiaguide.com/habitat/construction-kits); full list also at [Game8: List of All Kits](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/586481)

### Inferences
- A planner flag for "block-built" should be keyed per kit, not per size tier. Use the Bulbapedia note "made out of blocks … cannot be relocated or demolished" as the test. Orange hut and Log cabin are confirmed. Yellow cottage probably is too, given its loft and ladders, but that is not confirmed.
- A block-built kit should follow the block-house rules (the space enclosed by walls and a door, the size cap, the vertical range around the door; see section 3), not the interior-scene rules.

### Gaps
- No complete list sorting all house kits into "interior scene" and "block-built". The Game8 and Serebii kit lists could not be fetched.
- Whether every colour or theme variant of a size tier (leaf, stone, city, orange, yellow…) behaves the same way.
- Whether block-built kits count as roofed, and whether only their ground floor counts for environment. No source found.

## 2. Interior size, height and capacity per kit; can furniture be stacked?

### Takeaway
Interior kits have fixed furniture grids that grow by tier: den 4×3, hut 6×5, cottage 10×8, house 14×10 on each of 2 floors. Interior heights are known only for the City cottage (4) and the Poké Ball house (3). Furniture inside kits can be placed in half-tile steps. I found no source on stacking items on shelves or tables.

### Cited Findings
- [Confirmed] Sizes by tier. Den: 2×2 footprint, 4×3 interior, 1 floor, 1 Pokémon, small or medium only. Hut: 3×3 footprint, 6×5 interior, 1 floor, 1 Pokémon. Cottage: 5×4 footprint, 10×8 interior, 1 floor, 2 Pokémon. House: 8×6 footprint, 14×10 interior, 2 floors. — [The Games Wiki: How to Remodel a House](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/how-to-remodel-a-house)
- [Confirmed] Leaf house kit: "House 4 (2 per floor) Construction: 8x6 Exterior: 8x6 Interior: 2 floors, 14x10 each Next Day". — [Game8: How to Build Houses and Structures](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)
- [Unverified] Capacity of the 2-floor House: 4 per Game8 and The Games Wiki, 5 per a separate kit guide. — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771); [The Games Wiki: Building](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/building); [The Games Wiki: Building Kits Guide](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/building-kits-guide)
- [Confirmed] Leaf furniture grids, in tiles of width × depth where furniture can go: はっぱのおへや 4×3, こや 6×5, いえ 10×8, りっぱな "14×奥行10マス(1階2階とも)。かべかけ可" (14 wide by 10 deep on both floors; wall hangings allowed). — [pon-threetree: 建築キット一覧 はっぱ編](https://pon-threetree.com/pokopia-pokemon-nintendo-switch-architecture-2/)
- [Confirmed] Stone kits: いしのおへや 4×3 and いしのこや 6×5 furniture grids. いしのいえ needs a 5×5 build area; its grid size is not given. — [pon-threetree: 建築キット一覧 いし編](https://pon-threetree.com/pokopia-pokemon-nintendo-switch-architecture-stone-2/)
- [Confirmed] City cottage kit: "Max inhabitable 2 Interior size 10×8×4 Floors 1", interior items simple curtain (left) and simple curtain (right). — [Bulbapedia: City cottage kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/City_cottage_kit)
- [Confirmed] City house kit interior items: simple curtain (left), simple curtain (right), house partition (big). — [Serebii: City house kit](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/build/cityhousekit.shtml)
- [Confirmed] Leaf cottage: 10×8 interior, 1 floor, 2 residents. — [Bulbapedia: Leaf cottage kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Leaf_cottage_kit)
- [Unverified] Poké Ball house: Bulbapedia gives "interior size 5×5×3, floors 1", maximum 1 resident. The Games Wiki says 2. — [Bulbapedia: Poké Ball house kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Ball_house_kit); [The Games Wiki: Building](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/building)
- [Unverified] A GameFAQs entry lists an "Imperial House" with a 9×10 build size and a 2-storey 14×10 living space for up to 4 Pokémon. — [GameFAQs: Building Kits](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/switch-2/665508-pokemon-pokopia-bubbly-basin/faqs/82672/building-kits)
- [Community-tested] Inside a kit house, unlike outdoors, furniture can be placed in 0.5-tile steps ("家の中は屋外と違い、0.5マス単位で家具を設置することができる"). — [kyu49 (ぽこポケ学会): 家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)
- [Community-tested] A blog that builds interior templates calls the hut (6×5) cramped, and leaves out partitions there because they feel "圧迫感" (oppressive). — [東方錆恋録: 建物内装テンプレート考察](https://rusted-coil.hateblo.jp/entry/2026/04/04/151815)
- [Community-tested] On a two-floor kit, only the storage on the same floor as the crafting table can be reached from it. — [あしやひろ on X](https://x.com/kk6/status/2033171664570343921)

### Inferences
- In-game the interior grid is half-tile, so a top-down planner on whole tiles will slightly understate how densely a kit interior can be furnished.
- The interior heights we have (4 for the City cottage, 3 for the Poké Ball house) suggest a low ceiling, so a Z layer inside an interior kit could be capped at about 3 to 4.

### Gaps
- Whether small items can be placed on top of tables, shelves or counters inside kit interiors, and whether those items count for the 3-furniture rule, for favourites (rest, toy, decoration) or for environment. No source found in English or Japanese ("棚の上 家具 カウント" returned nothing relevant).
- Interior heights for the den, hut, house, Log cabin and the other variants.
- Whether furniture can be stacked inside interior kits.

## 3. Does the area around a kit house count, and how far horizontally and vertically?

### Takeaway
For houses in general, the area that counts for comfort can reach outside the walls. The game draws it as a white dotted outline when you press the right stick while a Pokémon lives there, and items outside count if Pokémon can reach them. One player measured the vertical reach as 4 tiles below to 3 tiles above the door. None of this was tested specifically on interior-scene kits, so the planner's guess of "4 tiles around the kit" is still unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- [Confirmed] "住心地に影響する範囲は、住んでいるポケモンがいる状態でRスティックを押し込むと、白い点線で表示される": the area that affects comfort shows as a white dotted line when you press the right stick while a resident is there. It may not show when the surrounding ground is at a very different height from the house floor. — [kyu49 (ぽこポケ学会): 家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)
- [Community-tested] Items Pokémon can't reach don't affect comfort ("ポケモンがアクセス不可能だと、住心地に影響を与えない"). — [kyu49: 家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)
- [Community-tested] "地表から5マス目までに扉があれば、地表上のアイテムは認識される": if the door is no higher than the 5th tile above ground level, items on the ground are still recognised. — [kyu49: 家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)
- [Community-tested] The vertical reach of furniture is −4 to +3 relative to the door ("家具の効果範囲の上下は 扉の位置を基準にして-４～+３"). The player says it was tested carefully. — [あめしこうR2 on X](https://x.com/Ameshikou_R2/status/2040302712747950405)
- [Community-tested] Comfort items can be placed outside the house, as long as they are inside the range and reachable. — reported in the search summary for [KYU on X](https://x.com/kyuphd/status/2030234005325668475) and [kyu49: 家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)
- [Community-tested] Comfort needs all three favourite kinds, rest (きゅうけい), toy (おもちゃ) and decoration (かざりつけ), to be inside the recognised range. Duplicates of the same furniture may not add more. — [kyu49: 家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)
- [Unverified] Players note that Pokémon living in kit houses rarely come outside, compared with those in block houses. — [Yahoo!知恵袋 q11327035231](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q11327035231)

### Inferences
- For block-built kits (Orange hut, Log cabin), the block-house rules above should apply directly. Model a range around the door with Z from −4 to +3, plus the reachability check.
- For interior-scene kits, the measurements may or may not carry over to the outside. The safe planner default is to show the outdoor ring as an unverified estimate.

### Gaps
- How far the range reaches horizontally outside either kind of house. No source gives a tile count; the dotted outline exists but nobody has published its size.
- Whether an interior-scene kit has any outdoor range at all, or whether its interior alone is assessed.
- Whether the −4/+3 vertical result was measured on a block house or a kit.

## 4. Two-floor kits: what counts for whom, and how are residents assigned?

### Takeaway
The 2-floor House holds 2 Pokémon per floor. You assign a floor by taking or inviting the Pokémon there. One player saw a Pokémon on an empty floor 2 at maximum comfort, which suggests both floors are judged together. I found no source for the planner's rule that the two pairs who share the most sleep end up on the same floor.

### Cited Findings
- [Confirmed] Leaf house kit capacity "4 (2 per floor)". — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)
- [Community-tested] Because the りっぱな kit takes 2 per floor, a Pokémon won't move in unless you take it to floor 2. One answer says you can move it in by inviting it from outside and choosing the floor, or by inviting it while on floor 2. — [Yahoo!知恵袋 q12327162557](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q12327162557); [Yahoo!知恵袋 q12326493027](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q12326493027)
- [Unverified] Asked whether furniture is judged per floor, the best answer was: "おそらく共通。何も置いてない２階のポケモンが住心地最高になってたんで" (probably shared, because a Pokémon on an empty floor 2 had top comfort). — [Yahoo!知恵袋 q11326372311](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q11326372311)
- [Community-tested] Layout: each floor of the りっぱな kit looks like 2 rooms, though they are not fully separated. Floor 2 has no back staircase and its full 14×10 can take two Pokémon with different tastes. — [lushgreengame: 家の比較](https://lushgreengame.com/pocopoke-house-hikaku/); [東方錆恋録](https://rusted-coil.hateblo.jp/entry/2026/04/04/151815)
- [Unverified] Pokémon sometimes sleep in a bed or chair on the loft. — [Yahoo!知恵袋 q12327667777](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q12327667777)
- [Unverified] A player's りっぱないえキット took only 2 Pokémon; the 3rd and later declined, saying the house would get crowded ("自分が引っ越したら狭くなるから遠慮する"). The player suspected a bug. — [reshika on X](https://x.com/reshika_cb/status/2031749064312897852)

### Inferences
- The 2-per-floor rule plus the "crowded" refusal suggest the limit is enforced per floor. The planner should model 2 slots on each floor rather than 4 shared slots.
- If comfort is judged across the whole house, floor-1 favourites would count for floor-2 residents and vice versa. That rests on a single observation.

### Gaps
- Whether favourites, environment or the 3-furniture rule are judged per floor or for the whole house. Only the one Yahoo observation exists.
- The rule for which Pokémon go together on a floor (sleep sharing or friendship). No source found.
- Whether items in the loft or on floor 2 of block-built kits such as the Yellow cottage count for ground-floor residents.

## 5. Wall decorations inside kits, and the furniture needed to move in

### Takeaway
Every source agrees a kit house needs 3 pieces of furniture before a Pokémon moves in. Japanese sources sharpen that to 3 *different kinds*. Wall hangings can be placed in the House tier, and wallpaper can be changed from the cottage tier up. I found nothing on whether wall items count toward the 3 or as favourites.

### Cited Findings
- [Confirmed] "Furnish the house with at least 3 furniture pieces if you want other Pokemon to live inside it." The house sparkles once it is habitable. If the sparkle doesn't appear, Game8 suggests using different furniture pieces. — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)
- [Community-tested] It is 3 different kinds: "同じ家具を3個置いても条件を満たしません" (3 of the same furniture doesn't meet it), and the same chair 3 times counts as 1 kind. — [gemukan: 家判定の完全ガイド](https://www.gemukan.com/pokoa-building-001/); [goccha-journal](https://goccha-journal.com/8814); [mutyun](https://www.mutyun.com/archives/262847.html)
- [Community-tested] Dropping below 3 while redecorating makes the building stop counting as a house ("3つ未満になると家として判定されなくなってしまう"). — [Game8 JP: 模様替えのやり方と注意点](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769142)
- [Confirmed] All furniture and Pokémon must be taken out before a house can be moved, demolished or remodelled. — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771); [The Games Wiki: How to Remodel a House](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/how-to-remodel-a-house)
- [Confirmed] The りっぱな (House) tier allows wall hangings ("かべかけ可"). — [pon-threetree はっぱ編](https://pon-threetree.com/pokopia-pokemon-nintendo-switch-architecture-2/)
- [Unverified] Wallpaper can be changed from the いえ (cottage) tier up. — [東方錆恋録](https://rusted-coil.hateblo.jp/entry/2026/04/04/151815) (per search summary; not seen in full)

### Inferences
- The planner's rule "3 furniture pieces" should read "3 different furniture kinds", and should warn when the 3 are duplicates.

### Gaps
- Whether wall hangings (posters, wall clocks, wall lights), wallpaper and flooring count toward the 3 kinds or as favourites. No source found.
- Whether the built-in interior items (curtains, partitions) count toward the 3.

## 6. Planner assumptions: confirm, refine or refute

### Takeaway
Most of the planner's assumptions cannot be checked from the sources I could reach. The 3-furniture rule should be refined to 3 kinds, 2-floor capacity is confirmed at 2 per floor, and the block-built kit list should be corrected. The other assumptions stay unverified.

### Cited Findings
- "Everything inside counts, on both floors and at any height": [Unverified]. One observation suggests the floors share a judgement. — [Yahoo!知恵袋 q11326372311](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q11326372311)
- "No daylight; windows, curtains and wallpaper don't count for light": [Unverified, sources disagree]. Game8 JP says a roofed house keeps its interior brightness whatever the outside light, and interior lighting stops it counting as dark. — [Game8 JP: 屋根の付け方](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768973); [Game8 JP: 暗い環境](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769395). One blog says kit houses come with a built-in interior effect that makes them bright automatically. A Yahoo question says kit houses are bright without electricity, while an answer there says they are basically dark. — [気ままなニュース: ブロック建築が暗い時](https://dokodeyomu.hatenadiary.com/entry/Pokoa_Pokemon_Block_Building); [Yahoo!知恵袋 q10328110095](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q10328110095)
- "3 furniture pieces before anyone moves in": refine to 3 *kinds* [Community-tested]. — [gemukan](https://www.gemukan.com/pokoa-building-001/); [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)
- "Cottages and log cabin are built from blocks": partly refuted. Bulbapedia confirms the Orange hut and Log cabin. The Yellow cottage is likely (it has a loft and ladders). City and Leaf cottages have interior items and look like interior kits. — [Bulbapedia: Orange hut kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Orange_hut_kit); [Bulbapedia: Log cabin kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Log_cabin_kit); [Bulbapedia: Yellow cottage kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/yellow_cottage_kit); [Bulbapedia: City cottage kit](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/City_cottage_kit)
- "Two-floor kits: residents assigned to floors": confirmed at 2 per floor [Confirmed]. The pairing-by-sleep rule is [Unverified, no source]. — [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)
- "Small kits can't hold large Pokémon": [Confirmed] for the den ("small or medium" only). — [The Games Wiki: How to Remodel a House](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/how-to-remodel-a-house). A Japanese guide says "建築キットで作った家はポケモンの体の大きさによって住める種類が決まっている" (which species can live in a kit house depends on body size). That line came from a search over several pages, and I could not pin it to one of them, e.g. [ポケモナンザ: 家判定されない原因](https://pokemonza.hatenablog.jp/entry/2026/03/12/073425) or [GameWith: 家の建て方](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545341), so treat it as [Unverified]. Not confirmed for the Poké Ball house.
- Size cap for block-built houses (relevant to block-built kits): [Unverified, sources disagree]. lushgreengame gives the largest room as 9×10. kyu49 is summarised as saying 9×10 and up isn't allowed. GameRant says no more than 100 square blocks, and that a big building can be split into several rooms, each with its own door. — [lushgreengame: 家判定の最小と最大](https://lushgreengame.com/pocopoke-kensyou-ookisa/); [kyu49](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/); [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/pokopia-big-house-size-limit/)

### Inferences
- Of the planner's kit-house assumptions, the ones with the most support are: 2 slots per floor, at least 3 distinct furniture kinds, a den-only size limit for large Pokémon, and a −4/+3 Z range around the door for block-built kits.

### Gaps
- Patch history: none of the sources mention a patch that changed kit-house rules.
- The pages that would most likely settle the open questions (kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/, Game8 kit pages, Serebii building pages) were blocked by this session's network allowlist. They need fetching in full once kyu49.github.io, game8.co, game8.jp, serebii.net and bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net are allowed.
