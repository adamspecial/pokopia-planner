# Habitats and range checks in Pokémon Pokopia: vertical (3D) rules

Labels used on every finding: **[Confirmed]** = official Nintendo/Game Freak/Pokémon Company text or an in-game UI element with a stated value; **[Community-tested]** = a player or guide site reports a measured result; **[Unverified]** = a guide's guess, an unclear source, or a single listicle. No finding below reached [Confirmed]: none of the sources found gives an official number for a vertical rule. What the game itself shows (the white wind effect, the white dotted range drawn when the R stick is pressed) is reported by guides, so those items are [Community-tested] with an in-game cue.

Source access note: during this research, WebFetch could not resolve any of these hosts: kyu49.github.io, game8.jp, gamepedia.jp, all0kay.com, thegameswiki.com, detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp. Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine result text (titles, snippets and the search tool's summaries of those pages), not from reading the full pages. Where the search summary did not make clear which page a sentence came from, the finding says so. Each number should be checked on the page itself before the planner relies on it.

The Japanese title is ぽこ あ ポケモン (ぽこポケ). Japanese guides count blocks/tiles in マス (tiles) and 段 (layers/steps).

## Do habitat recipes need all parts on the same level? Is there a vertical tolerance for habitat parts and for the area that belongs to a habitat?

### Takeaway
No source gives a numeric vertical tolerance for habitat parts. Japanese guides consistently report that height differences (段差) between parts can stop a habitat from being recognized, and advise putting every part on the same plane with no gaps. One player test found that a block placed directly above a habitat's grass did not break it. The planner's "±4 levels from the habitat base" rule for habitat parts has no supporting source. A ±4 value does appear for other checks (see the house and request-furniture questions), so it may have been borrowed from them.

### Cited Findings
- [Community-tested] A troubleshooting guide says a habitat may not be recognized when its parts sit at different heights (段差). In the reported case, the habitat was recognized once the furniture was moved onto the floor. The guide's advice is to put parts on the same plane as far as possible (できるだけ同じ平面に置き) and leave no gaps between items (アイテムの間を開けない). — [all0kay.com hint guide](https://all0kay.com/poco-a-pokemon-hint-trouble/) (snippet; the page could not be opened)
- [Community-tested] Another search summary, citing the same group of guides, repeats this advice: when a habitat is not recognized, check whether the heights have drifted out of line (高さがズレていないか) and put the parts on the same plane. — [all0kay.com](https://all0kay.com/poco-a-pokemon-hint-trouble/); [kengomon habitat guide](https://www.kengomon.com/pokoa-pokemon-livepokemonmake/) (search summary; it is unclear which of the two pages the sentence came from)
- [Community-tested] Placement does not have to match a layout block for block (配置は完全一致じゃなくてもOK; ブロック単位でピッタリ同じ位置に置かなきゃダメ、みたいなことはない). — [kengomon placement guide](https://www.kengomon.com/%E3%80%90%E3%81%BD%E3%81%93%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%80%91%E7%94%9F%E6%81%AF%E5%9C%B0%E3%81%AE%E5%8A%B9%E7%8E%87%E7%9A%84%E3%81%AA%E3%81%BE%E3%81%A8%E3%82%81%E6%96%B9%E3%83%BB%E9%85%8D%E7%BD%AE%E5%AE%8C/) (snippet)
- [Community-tested] Several similar habitats close together can interfere with each other's recognition (判定が干渉), so guides advise leaving some space between them. — [kengomon placement guide](https://www.kengomon.com/%E3%80%90%E3%81%BD%E3%81%93%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%80%91%E7%94%9F%E6%81%AF%E5%9C%B0%E3%81%AE%E5%8A%B9%E7%8E%87%E7%9A%84%E3%81%AA%E3%81%BE%E3%81%A8%E3%82%81%E6%96%B9%E3%83%BB%E9%85%8D%E7%BD%AE%E5%AE%8C/) (snippet)
- [Unverified] One guide recommends always keeping the tiles within 5 of a habitat clear (生息地の周囲5マス以内は必ず空けておく). No vertical extent is given. — search summary for "生息地 白い点線 範囲"; the exact page was not named, but candidates in that result set were [bladevi recipe list](https://bladevi.com/habitat-pocoapokemon/) and [pokemongo-raku list](https://pokemongo-raku.com/post147489)
- [Community-tested] Many recipes count as complete once 4 tiles are connected (4マス繋がっていればOK), for example 4 tall grass for a tall-grass habitat. — [AppMedia habitat dex](https://appmedia.jp/pocoapokemon/79811225) (snippet)
- [Community-tested] One player test found that placing a block directly above a dwelling's grass still left it recognized as a dwelling (すみかの草の直上にブロック置いてもすみか判定でした). — [X post by @__ririmero](https://x.com/__ririmero/status/2029761931813474412)
- [Community-tested] A habitat built inside a house still triggers the dwelling check (ポケモンの生息地は、家の中に作られていても住居判定が発生する). — search summary for "生息地 判定 範囲"; the source page was not named (candidates include [kengomon](https://www.kengomon.com/pokoa-pokemon-livepokemonmake/) and [nasu66](https://nasu66.com/entry/poco-a-pokemon-habitat-guide))
- [Community-tested] Recipes with an "elevated"/"high-up location" part, such as Elevated Tall Grass (#006: 4 tall grass + a high-up location), need the grass itself to be on high ground. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/wikis/pokopia/elevated-tall-grass-habitat/); [pokopiahabitats.com](https://pokopiahabitats.com/habitat/elevated-tall-grass); [Serebii habitat page](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/habitatdex/elevatedtallgrass.shtml)
- [Community-tested] Sources disagree on whether Elevated Yellow Tall Grass needs a separate high-up-location part. GameSpot says 4 yellow tall grass on elevated land; The Games Wiki lists only 4 yellow tall grass. — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/gallery/pokemon-pokopia-habitat-dex-how-to-create-all-habitats/2900-7560/); contradicted by [The Games Wiki](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/elevated-ground)
- [Community-tested] Each habitat has an area that the game draws as a white dotted line. A sorting guide uses that dotted area as the anchor for its range measurements (see the community box question). — [nomanoma-web sorting guide](https://nomanoma-web.com/2026/03/%E3%80%90%E3%81%BD%E3%81%93%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%80%91%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%81%8C%E3%81%97%E3%82%8F%E3%81%91%E3%81%A6%E3%81%8F%E3%82%8C%E3%81%AA%E3%81%84%E5%8E%9F%E5%9B%A0%EF%BC%81/) (snippet)
- [Community-tested] Pressing the R stick near a dwelling (すみか) shows its range. — [Game8 JP Hoppip page](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768742) (search summary)

### Inferences
- The safest model is a strict one: all recipe parts on the same surface level, contiguous, with a block allowed directly above grass. The planner's ±4 tolerance for habitat parts is not supported and would likely report habitats as valid when the game rejects them.
- In the planner, "same plane" most likely means the same top surface (the same Z for the base of each item and tile), not merely "within ±N".
- A block placed above grass not breaking a dwelling suggests that blocks stacked above a habitat do not block recognition. Treat this as a single test, not a general rule.

### Gaps
- No numeric vertical tolerance (for example ±1 or ±2) for habitat parts was found. A side-by-side test (grass on level 0 plus an item on level 1) is not documented in any source reached.
- No source says how tall the habitat's white-dotted area is, or which Z it is measured from.
- The ぽこポケ学会 site (kyu49.github.io) could not be read, so it is unknown whether it has a habitat height page. Search only surfaced its house, design, limitation, circuit and harvest pages.

## Can habitat items (bench, lamp, rock) on top of blocks or walls count as part of a recipe?

### Takeaway
No source tests this directly. The only evidence is the advice above: a habitat failed until its furniture was moved down onto the floor. That points to items raised on blocks not combining with grass on the ground. Treat it as unknown, leaning towards "doesn't count".

### Cited Findings
- [Community-tested] A furniture item left off the ground plane stopped a habitat from being recognized, and moving it onto the floor fixed it. — [all0kay.com](https://all0kay.com/poco-a-pokemon-hint-trouble/) (snippet)
- [Community-tested] Planters count as furniture for the dry-rock check: they do not block the effect, and plants in planters are affected when they are within range. This shows that at least one system treats furniture on a block differently from blocks. — [X post by @aminisi](https://x.com/aminisi/status/2037509264559030467)
- [Community-tested] Habitat requirements can include blocks, objects, arrangements, weather and times of day, so a habitat can also fail for reasons other than height. — [Pokodex app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6760185010)

### Inferences
- A planner should warn, not hard-fail, when a recipe item sits higher than the grass or tiles it is meant to combine with.

### Gaps
- No source covers items on walls, items on top of other furniture, or items on half-height surfaces.

## Water, lava and grass at height or depth (waterfalls, raised water)

### Takeaway
Water can be placed at a chosen height but must be attached to something. It has a fill-level setting within a block, and a waterfall forms on its own when water is released from a higher point. Guides report a minimum waterfall height of 4 or 5 blocks; the figures conflict and the source is uncertain. A river dug 1 tile down is reported to count as a water source.

### Cited Findings
- [Community-tested] Holding ZL adjusts the height at which water is placed, but the water must be attached to something. A water-level setting changes how much of the block the water fills. On the low setting, a puddle spreads in a circle 7 blocks wide. — [Serebii water page](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/water.shtml) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] When funnelled between walls, one drop of water travels about 25 blocks along a trench; lava travels about 12. — [Serebii water page](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/water.shtml) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] To make a waterfall, carry water up to a higher point and release it; gravity forms the waterfall. Waterfalls are required for the Training Waterfall and Waterwheel Spot habitats. — [RankedBoost](https://rankedboost.com/pokopia/guides/how-to-place-water/) (search summary)
- [Unverified] A waterfall (滝) needs to be at least 5 tiles tall to count (最低5マス分の高さがあればOK). — search summary for "生息地 判定 範囲 何マス 高さ"; the source page was not named (the result set included [gamepedia.jp habitat tips](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4039) and [all0kay.com](https://all0kay.com/poco-a-pokemon-hint-trouble/)). Contradicted by [pokopia.center](https://pokopia.center/posts/pokopia-all-habitats-pokemon-list-2026/), which says Waterfall Grotto needs a vertical water feature at least 4 blocks tall.
- [Unverified] A river dug 1 tile down makes the lowered tile count as a water source (川は1マス掘り下げると、その下がった場所が水源として判定される). — search summary for "生息地 高さ 段差"; the source page was not named (the result set included [GameWith high-ground page](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/549482) and [Game8 JP high-ground page](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769075))
- [Community-tested] A guide covers how to make sea water, hot springs, high ground and stumps for habitats. — [gamepedia.jp habitat tips](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4039) (title only)

### Inferences
- The planner should store a water tile's surface Z separately from its source block, and treat a waterfall as a vertical run of water of at least 4–5 blocks.
- Partial fill levels mean the water surface is not always a whole block. The planner may only need to know whether a tile has water.

### Gaps
- No source says whether tall grass or flowers count when planted on raised terrain next to ground-level grass.
- No source says whether lava or water in a raised basin counts towards a habitat at ground level.
- No source gives a minimum height for the Waterwheel Spot or Illuminated Waterfall habitats ([Game8 EN Illuminated Waterfall](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/586067) appeared in search, but the snippet had no number).

## What counts as "nearby" for items a Pokémon likes (house comfort and request furniture)? Horizontal radius and vertical tolerance

### Takeaway
For house comfort, ぽこポケ学会 (kyu49) measures height from the door (0) and counts items from 4 levels below to 3 above. Items outside the house count if they are in range and reachable. This matches the planner's "4 below to 3 above the door". One player test says furniture for a Pokémon's request counts up to 4 tiles above or below. No source gave a horizontal radius for the comfort area; the planner's "4 tiles past the walls" did not come up in any source reached.

### Cited Findings
- [Community-tested, in-game cue] With a resident living in a house, pressing the R stick draws the comfort range as a white dotted line. It may not be drawn when the surrounding ground is at a very different height from the house floor. — [kyu49 "家と住み心地"](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] Heights count from the door: the door's height is 0, items up to 3 tiles above are recognized, and the full recognized band is −4 to +3 from the door. — [kyu49 "家と住み心地"](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] Items that improve comfort may sit outside the house if they are within range. They must be reachable, and they may be at a different height from the house, but they are not recognized if the height or the walking distance needed to reach them is too great. — [KYU on X](https://x.com/kyuphd/status/2030234005325668475) (search summary of the thread)
- [Community-tested] Two houses can share the same furniture, including furniture inside a house. — [kyu49 "家と住み心地"](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] What counts as a house: a floor of at least 2×2 (1×4 does not count), enclosed by walls at least 1 block high, with at least one door (2-tile doors are OK). Glass or even fences can serve as walls. — [KYU on X](https://x.com/kyuphd/status/2030234005325668475)
- [Community-tested] Furniture for a request (おねがいごと家具) counts up to 4 tiles above or below (上下4マス目の距離までOK). An empty house needs a floor of at least 4 tiles and a door, works without a ceiling, and its walls can be 1 block high. — [X post by @__ririmero](https://x.com/__ririmero/status/2029761931813474412)
- [Community-tested] Reported house sizes: largest 9×10 and smallest 2×2 ([lushgreengame](https://lushgreengame.com/pocopoke-kensyou-ookisa/)); contradicted by a largest enclosure of about 12 wide × 11 deep ([X post by @Next_gamer](https://x.com/Next_gamer/status/2031298987429605435)). The second figure may include the walls.
- [Community-tested] Game8 JP and GameWith both have pages on what makes a block-built house count. — [Game8 JP](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768198); [GameWith](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547952); [Altema](https://altema.jp/pocoapokemon/iehantei) (titles only)

### Inferences
- The kyu49 band (−4 to +3 from the door) supports the planner's house rule. The ±4 in the @__ririmero test may have been counted from the floor, or may count the 4th tile inclusively, so it is not a clear contradiction.
- The "reachable" requirement means a pure geometry check can report items as counting when the game does not. The planner could warn when an item has no walkable path to the house.

### Gaps
- No source gave the horizontal distance (tiles past the walls) for the comfort range.
- No source says whether items on a different floor of a multi-storey building count.
- No source covers vertical rules for a Pokémon's liked items around a habitat (as opposed to a house).

## Community box and other range-based mechanics (sorting, sprinklers, dry rock, Yawn, lights)

### Takeaway
The best-documented vertical range is for the sorting specialty (しわける). The community box (みんなのボックス) must sit within 4 tiles horizontally of the sorting Pokémon's habitat (its white dotted area), and within 10 above or 8 below it, entirely inside that box-shaped area. Sprinklers water a diamond of radius 5 and only use water at the sprinkler's own height. Dry rock (さらさらいわ) acts only on the highest block in its range, which runs from 1 above to 2 below the rock. Nothing was found on Slowpoke/Yawn or lights.

### Cited Findings
- [Community-tested] Sorting works when a community box is within 4 tiles horizontally, 10 above and 8 below the habitat's white dotted area (生息地（白点線の範囲）から縦横＋4マス、上＋10マス、下＋8マス以内). If even 1 tile of the box sticks out of that area, it does not work. — [nomanoma-web sorting guide](https://nomanoma-web.com/2026/03/%E3%80%90%E3%81%BD%E3%81%93%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%80%91%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%81%8C%E3%81%97%E3%82%8F%E3%81%91%E3%81%A6%E3%81%8F%E3%82%8C%E3%81%AA%E3%81%84%E5%8E%9F%E5%9B%A0%EF%BC%81/) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] The same figures (4 across, 10 up, 8 down from the habitat; no part outside), with more test results: with several boxes in range only one is used; the Pokémon picks up materials it can see, apparently with no distance limit; it collected through a wall from up to 7 tiles away; and it reached a box 1 tile higher. — [X post by @winter8636](https://x.com/winter8636/status/2037171863265353976)
- [Community-tested] Sorting Pokémon carry items lying on the map to a nearby community box, and also carry materials from the box to processing machines. — [Game8 JP sorting page](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767539); [Altema](https://altema.jp/pocoapokemon/tokui/17) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] Beyond a certain distance, sorting Pokémon do not notice an item. — [Yahoo!知恵袋](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q14326783204) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] Guides suggest grouping the habitats of littering (ちらかす) and sorting (しわける) Pokémon close together and placing a community box. — [Famitsu tips](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202603/68730) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] A sprinkler's range is a diamond of radius 5 (Manhattan distance 5), so the corners of a square plot are left dry. — [X post by @Hikikomori_1](https://x.com/Hikikomori_1/status/2031921676318052603); [game9820](https://game9820.com/pocoa-3111150/). GameWith describes this as spreading over 11×11 and watering 61 tiles in total ([GameWith sprinkler](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/548144)). A diamond of radius 5 holds 61 tiles. Other figures conflict: "36 blocks" ([hyperts](https://hyperts.net/pokopoke/farming/)) and "max 5×5, corners not watered" ([piyo-piyo04](https://piyo-piyo04.com/2026/03/21/%E3%80%90%E3%81%BD%E3%81%93%E3%81%82%E3%80%91%E3%82%B9%E3%83%97%E3%83%AA%E3%83%B3%E3%82%AF%E3%83%A9%E3%83%BC%E5%85%A5%E6%89%8B%E3%83%BB%E9%87%8F%E7%94%A3%E3%83%BB%E7%BD%AE%E3%81%8D%E6%96%B9%E3%81%AE/)).
- [Community-tested] A sprinkler needs a water source within its range. Water that is in range but at a different height from the sprinkler does not work (高さが違う場所にあると機能しません). — search summary for "スプリンクラー 範囲 高さ"; most likely [Game8 JP sprinkler](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769157) or [GameWith sprinkler](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/548144) (the source was not named)
- [Community-tested] Guides recommend burying sprinklers under the farm, which also stops them being picked up by mistake during harvest. One layout digs 2 tiles down at the 3rd tile from each corner, places a sprinkler, then tills. — [piyo-piyo04 underground sprinkler](https://piyo-piyo04.com/2026/06/04/%E3%80%90%E3%81%BD%E3%81%93%E3%81%82%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%80%91%E3%82%B9%E3%83%97%E3%83%AA%E3%83%B3%E3%82%AF%E3%83%A9%E3%83%BC%E3%81%AF%E5%9C%B0%E4%B8%8B%E8%A8%AD%E7%BD%AE%E3%81%8C/); [lifematome sprinkler](https://lifematome.blog/pokopoke-sprincler-hatake/) (search summary; which page gave which detail is unclear)
- [Community-tested] Dry rock (さらさらいわ) acts only on the highest block in its range: with a block at +1, nothing at 0 to −2 dries. Planters count as furniture, so they do not block it and what is below them still dries. Plants in planters dry if they are within +1 to −2. Trees do not block it either. — [X post by @aminisi](https://x.com/aminisi/status/2037509264559030467)
- [Unverified] Sprinklers reportedly share dry rock's vertical range and behaviour, so planters let one sprinkler and one dry rock switch a two-layer farm between wet and dry. — search summary for "スプリンクラー 地下設置 さらさらいわ"; the source was not named (candidates: [note.com qoo_estel](https://note.com/qoo_estel/n/n4f10ad36fe8a), [X @aminisi](https://x.com/aminisi/status/2037509264559030467))
- [Community-tested] To run dry rock reliably, keep it at least 11 tiles from interfering sources. — [note.com qoo_estel](https://note.com/qoo_estel/n/n4f10ad36fe8a) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] A guide titled "dry rock effect range: how many tiles up, down, left and right does it dry?" exists. — [nomanoma-web さらさらいわ](https://nomanoma-web.com/2026/03/%E3%80%90%E3%81%BD%E3%81%93%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%80%91%E3%81%95%E3%82%89%E3%81%95%E3%82%89%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%81%AE%E5%8A%B9%E6%9E%9C%E7%AF%84%E5%9B%B2%E3%81%BE%E3%81%A8%E3%82%81%EF%BC%81%E4%B8%8A/) (title only; the numbers were not seen)

### Inferences
- The planner's community box rule ("within 4 tiles of a home's active area") should be anchored to the sorting Pokémon's habitat area, not the house, with a vertical window of +10/−8 rather than ±4. The box must be wholly inside the area.
- The planner names the specialty "Gather". No source confirmed the official English name for しわける (Game8 JP's page gives only the Japanese name), so the planner should check that its "Gather" is しわける.
- A sprinkler needs its water at the same Z. Model its range as Manhattan distance ≤5 across, with a vertical band of perhaps +1/−2 (from dry rock, by analogy; unverified).

### Gaps
- No data was found on Slowpoke's Yawn (あくび) range or town-score effects, in English or Japanese.
- No data was found on light or lamp effect ranges, or their vertical limits.
- The horizontal size of the habitat's white dotted area (the anchor for the +4) is not documented.

## How is elevation measured in the game? Block units; does a slab or half-block exist?

### Takeaway
Elevation is counted in whole blocks (マス/段). For high ground (高いところ), the threshold is counted from a per-town baseline, not from the local ground, and the game shows white wind when you are high enough. Sources disagree on both the baseline (the Pokémon Center's height or the unbreakable map floor) and the numbers. No source mentions slabs or half-blocks; only water has partial fill levels.

### Cited Findings
- [Community-tested, in-game cue] A white wind effect appears at or above the height that counts as high ground. Because the required height differs by town, guides advise checking for the wind before placing items. — [Game8 JP high ground](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769075); [GameWith high ground](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/549482) (search summary); [Elevated Ground wiki](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/elevated-ground) (search summary)
- [Community-tested] Game8 JP: what counts is height above each map's lower limit, not how high a spot looks. The lower limit is the blocks that even powered-up Rock Smash cannot break. The baseline differs by town, and in Sparkling Skylands (キラキラうきしま) each floating island has its own. — [Game8 JP high ground](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769075) (search summaries). One summary also attributed "about 45+ tiles from the lower limit" to Game8. That figure was not seen in a snippet and conflicts with every other source, so treat it as [Unverified].
- [Community-tested] 攻略大百科's tests count from the Pokémon Center's height: Withered Wasteland (パサパサこうや) 5, Bleak Beach (ドンヨリうみべ) 11, Rocky Ridges (ゴツゴツやま) 9, Sparkling Skylands 3, Palette Town (まっさらの街) 12. — [gamepedia.jp habitat tips](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4039) (search summary; most likely source page)
- [Community-tested] Another site lists Rocky Ridges as 10 and Palette Town as 11. — search summary (same result set; the page was not named). The English wiki agrees: Sparkling Skylands 3, Withered Wasteland 5, Rocky Ridges 10, Bleak Beach 11, Palette Town 11, reached by stacking blocks or using natural cliffs. — [Elevated Ground wiki](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/elevated-ground) (search summary)
- [Unverified] AppMedia guesses that high ground is about 7 tiles up from the base (拠点から上方向7マス程度で達成？). It is written with a question mark. — [AppMedia habitat dex](https://appmedia.jp/pocoapokemon/79811225) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The planner's 0–9 elevation scale is a relative scale. High ground cannot be decided from the planner's level alone; it needs a per-town offset to the game's baseline. Thresholds of 3–12 blocks above the Pokémon Center level would mean many towns cannot reach high ground within 0–9 unless the base level is set above the Pokémon Center.
- A Pokémon Center baseline and a map-floor baseline may both hold, if each town's Pokémon Center sits at a fixed height above its map floor. That would explain why the counts differ between sources.
- With no evidence of slabs, integer Z per block is the right model; water fill level can be ignored or stored as a flag.

### Gaps
- No source confirms whether slabs, half-blocks or stairs change height.
- The map's maximum build height and depth, and the building limits, were not read (kyu49 limitation page and note.com both unreachable).
- No patch notes on changes to height or range rules were found. Sources are dated March–June 2026; no later revisions were seen.
