# Residents' comfort ranks power Pokopia's progression

Pokémon Pokopia's hidden progression runs through its residents, so the model a tile planner most needs, and does not yet have, is a per-town ledger of who lives where and how comfortable each one is. Each resident's comfort rank converts to fixed points (**Awesome 10, Great 5, Nice 2, Average 1, Iffy and homeless 0, doubled for legendary and mythical Pokémon**), and a town's total sets its environment level on a ladder of **3, 12, 26, 52, 77, 100, 120, 140 and 160 points for levels 2 to 10**. That level in turn gates shop kits, recipes, materials, story requests, Dive and Bubbly Basin, and two independent community tests agree on every value. Residents come from habitats that work as a yes-or-no recipe test holding **exactly one resident each**, in a save that allows **one of each species**, through a spawn lottery that favours unmet, commoner species and filters each Pokémon by time band, weather and town, with a dark or brightly lit habitat overriding the clock. Comfort is a six-step rank that moves only when a Pokémon notices a change and falls only reluctantly; it rewards a rest item, a toy, a decoration, favourite-flavour food and a liked environment. Specialties mostly act only when Ditto asks: nine run from home without Ditto (Litter, Gather, Grow, Water with a basin, Generate, and Burn, Chop, Crush and Recycle processing at a nearby box), and apart from Litter's drop ring and Gather's box reach, none has a published tile range. Everything runs on the console clock with a **05:00 reset**, kits finish after 15 minutes, an hour or at the next 05:00, and only **about 21 to 25 Pokémon appear in a town at once**, which caps any automation layout. Almost none of this is official or datamined. It rests on fan tests, the ぽこポケ学会 wiki and guide sites read in full, plus many search summaries, so contested rules should ship as editable settings. Checked against those sources, data.json is missing the three Apples to Applin habitats and their five Pokémon, gives concrete a 1:1 yield where guides give 1:4, misdescribes Chop, Crush, Bulldoze and Build, leaves out helper specialties for most block-built and landmark kits, and calls Palette Town post-story.

## Habitats are a yes-or-no test that hold one resident each

*Evidence base for the whole report.* Labels follow the research brief. **Official** means Nintendo text, in-game text or patch notes, here mostly as relayed by Serebii, Game8 and 攻略大百科 (gamepedia.jp). **Community-tested** means a player measured it and published the test. **Guide** means a guide site states it without a test, even where the page was read in full. **Unverified** covers single posts and pages seen only as search summaries. No datamine of Pokopia's parameter tables turned up in English or Japanese ([攻略大百科 Ver.2.0.0](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/5548)), so no rule below is datamined. The current version is **Ver. 2.0.1 (8 Sep 2026)**, a bug-fix patch, so the rules in force are those of Ver. 2.0.0 ([攻略大百科 最新アプデ](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3507)).

### A formed habitat is binary, and its resident stops all further spawns

A habitat counts once every recipe item stands together and every item marked with a power symbol is powered. The game confirms it with sparkles and a new entry in the "ちかくの生息地" (nearby habitats) panel at the top left. A habitat can still fail with every item present if the layout is off or a powered item has no power ([攻略大百科 3417](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3417)). **Nothing spawns at the moment of forming**: the Pokémon "appears after a certain time has passed", announced by rustling grass or rippling water, although some habitats produce their Pokémon directly, marked with a yellow speech bubble ([Game8 JP 768257](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768257)). A structure larger than 11×12 stops counting as a habitat [unverified, search summary] ([Serebii building](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/building.shtml)). **No source supports a bonus for extra ingredients.** A search summary credited Game8 JP with saying that placing a little more than the minimum raises the rate, but a full read of that page on 23 Nov 2026 found no such claim ([Game8 JP 767516](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767516)). The planner should treat a habitat as formed or not, and require power for powered parts.

**Each habitat houses one Pokémon, and once a Pokémon has made it home, nothing new spawns there until that resident moves out** [guide] ([攻略大百科 3417](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3417); [Game8 JP すみか移動](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767983)). Moving the resident into a house frees the habitat for the next species. A habitat built inside a block house is a home of its own: a 2×2 house holding the one-Pokémon habitats "Surging psychic power" (simple cushion + crystal ball) and "Lazy photo-album scrolling" (tablet + party cup) houses **4 + 2** Pokémon [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). **A save holds only one of each species.** Forms count separately and can live side by side: pale Pikachu and Pikachu, Mosslax and Snorlax, Professor Tangrowth and Tangrowth, West and East Shellos and Gastrodon, Amped and Low Key Toxtricity [guide] ([Game8 JP 769001](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769001)). Pokopia has no evolution mechanic, so every evolved stage is its own species with its own habitat slots, often the same habitat as its base form with a higher rarity ([Game8 JP 769013](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769013); [Serebii Hydrated tall grass](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/habitatdex/hydratedtallgrass.shtml)). One aggregator's claim that duplicate habitats bring extra copies of a species contradicts the one-per-species rule and should be ignored ([pokopia.center](https://pokopia.center/posts/pokopia-all-habitats-pokemon-list-2026/)).

### The lottery favours unmet, commoner species, and Mosslax is the only booster

Serebii's habitat dex has 257 habitats (209 Main, 36 Basin, 12 Event) and 504 Pokémon slots. A parse of all 257 pages finds **296 Common, 174 Rare and 27 Very Rare slots**, plus 7 blanks and one typo. 109 habitats hold one Pokémon, 82 hold two, 44 three, 14 four, 5 five and 3 six, and 134 of the 359 distinct names appear in more than one habitat ([Serebii Habitats](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/habitats.shtml)). Rarity belongs to a slot, not a species: Buizel, Corsola and Munchlax are Common in one habitat and Rare in another, and Charizard, Clefable and Kingdra are Rare in one and Very Rare in another ([Serebii Tall grass](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/habitatdex/tallgrass.shtml); [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/pokemon-pokopia/habitats)). **No source publishes probabilities** ([Deltia's](https://deltiasgaming.com/pokemon-rarity-in-pokemon-pokopia-explained/)), and the percentages on a fan spawn calculator look invented. What guides agree on is the ordering. Among a habitat's unmet species the lower tiers come first, a befriended species is never attracted again, the last unmet species is effectively certain, and **a habitat whose species have all appeared spawns nothing new** [guide] ([Altema](https://altema.jp/pocoapokemon/reapokemon); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/guide/pokemon-pokopia-how-to-evolve/); [Game8 JP 767516](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767516)). The only documented booster is a **spicy offering to Mosslax**, which raises the chance of Pokémon appearing at habitats in three strengths. A raw ingredient gives the weakest boost and a named cooked dish the strongest, the offering works once a day after the Bleak Beach story, and it lasts until the 05:00 reset ([Serebii Mosslax boosts](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/mosslaxboosts.shtml); [GameWith 547741](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547741)).

Timing is just as vague. Spawn checks run at random within fixed intervals, and higher-star Pokémon take longer ([Game8 JP 768257](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768257); [攻略大百科 3417](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3417)). First visitors usually arrive by the next morning, and rare ones take days [guide] ([RankedBoost](https://rankedboost.com/pokopia/guides/how-to-attract-pokemon/)). **Moving the console clock past a day makes a waiting Pokémon appear "一瞬で" (instantly)**; if it does not, the time band or weather is probably wrong ([Game8 JP 767516](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767516); [Game8 JP 767333](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767333)). Ver. 1.0.4 fixed a bug in which "if certain Pokémon were waiting to appear, other Pokémon would sometimes also fail to appear" [official] ([Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)), and guides warn that a spawn flag already set on one copy of a habitat can leave another copy empty ([攻略大百科 3417](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3417)). Together these suggest each town keeps a queue of pending arrivals. The planner should therefore show rarity tiers and the unmet-first order, never percentages, and flag habitats whose every species is already befriended as dead.

Two details matter for layout. First, **light overrides time**: "Night exclusive Pokémon will spawn if their habitat is in a place that's dark, such as a cave or underground and Day exclusive Pokémon will spawn at night if their planned habitat is surrounded by bright light" [guide] ([Serebii Time & Day](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/timeday.shtml)), and Ver. 1.1.0 fixed "Pokémon's active time not extending even in their preferred brightness" [official] ([Game8 JP アップデート](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/771132)). A planner that already scores a home's light can therefore mark a night-only Pokémon as available at any hour when its habitat scores dark. Second, **weather is not a clock**. It is a per-town state the player sets with Rain Dance and Sunny Day spots, and it holds until another spot changes it ([攻略大百科 天気](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4979); [Game8 天気](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768195)). The ten weather-limited slots are thus planner-controllable. In Bubbly Basin, **no Main-habitat slot lists the Basin as a location**, while Basin habitats list all five towns, Cloud Island and the Basin, Floatzel at Marine shop excepted ([Serebii Basin tall grass](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/habitatdex/basintallgrass.shtml); [Serebii Marine shop](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/habitatdex/marineshop.shtml)). A Main habitat planned in the Basin should carry a warning.

The sources disagree on a few slots. Serebii lists Charmeleon at Campsite as Common in all weather, while Game8 EN (updated 12 Aug 2026) has it Rare, sun and cloud only ([Serebii Campsite](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/habitatdex/campsite.shtml); [Game8 EN Campsite](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585071)). A Game8 EN summary limits Vespiquen at Field of Flowers to Withered Wasteland, which Serebii does not [unverified] ([Game8 EN](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585074)). Game8 JP's night-only list adds Illumise, the Litwick line, Ariados, Politoed and Dragapult, and its day-only list adds Bellsprout, Machoke, Popplio, Brionne, Stunfisk, Frillish, Jellicent and Luvdisc, none of which Serebii's slots restrict ([Game8 JP 769153](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769153)). Environment level and story progress also unlock some Pokémon ([GameWith 545345](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545345)), but no source lists which ones.

### Crowded habitats block each other, and only 21 to 25 Pokémon appear at once

Packing habitats close together can stop the wanted one from spawning, so guides advise removing unneeded habitats nearby ([攻略大百科 3417](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3417)). One blog pins the trigger to another habitat or home inside the R-stick dotted range ([dokodeyomu](https://dokodeyomu.hatenadiary.com/entry/Habitat_of_Pokoa_Pok%C3%A9mon)), and a "9-space rule" circulates without a pinned source [unverified] ([finalboss.io](https://finalboss.io/pokemon-pokopia-how-to-use-habitats-and-rain)). Duplicates raise throughput, one resident per copy, but players with many identical copies report long waits ([GameFAQs board](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/559184-pokemon-pokopia/81152779)), so the practical rule is **one copy per unmet species, spread across towns**. The ぽこポケ学会 limits page lists caps for power, crops, trees, storage, kits and rugs but **no cap on habitats per town** ([ぽこポケ学会 色々な上限](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)).

The display cap is a harder constraint. 攻略大百科 says "about 25 Pokémon are shown at once per area", that residents of kit houses that load a separate interior do not count, and that above 25 homeless Pokémon may not appear at all ([攻略大百科 3417](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3417)). A player with 280 hours measured **about 21** as the practical limit, with 20 to 25 the observed range ([Splatoonブキ研究所, 10 Apr](https://note.com/splatoonweapon/n/n0c7d1da22923)), and Game8 EN gives 22 to 24 per biome ([Game8 EN 585501](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585501)). Which residents appear is random. Undisplayed ones still exist and **keep Littering**, with excess drops stocked internally, but they cannot be found with Search, Fly or Teleport, and **Gather works only while its Pokémon is displayed** ([Splatoonブキ研究所](https://note.com/splatoonweapon/n/n0c7d1da22923)). Kit-house residents go indoors to sleep, which frees display slots at night ([ぽこポケ学会 ちらかす](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/)). Honey at a missing Pokémon's habitat brings it back at once, but "may also cause other Pokémon to despawn to maintain the maximum number", and homeless Pokémon cannot be summoned this way ([Game8 EN 585501](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585501)). Each town also allows **40 kit houses** (a two-floor house that loads a second map counts as 2) and **14 builds in progress at once** [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 色々な上限](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)). The planner should warn when a town's residents exceed about 21 outside kit houses, and should treat Gather-based automation as unreliable beyond that.

### Breaking a habitat is safe since Ver. 2.0.0

Breaking the habitat of a Pokémon not yet befriended leaves it homeless (すみかなし). It wanders the town if the town holds 25 or fewer Pokémon, and rebuilding the same habitat nearby brings it back ([攻略大百科 3417](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3417)). **Since Ver. 2.0.0 this is official**: "Even if a Pokémon's habitat is destroyed, it will move back in if the same habitat is rebuilt within a certain area" ([Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)). How far "a certain area" reaches is undocumented. Befriended residents do not vanish. A planner can therefore let users move habitats freely, at the cost of a short wait or a jar of Honey.

### Legendaries and event Pokémon sit outside the habitat lottery

There are **no shinies**. The base game's 12 legendary and mythical Pokémon come from the story, special kits, items and Dream Islands ([Game8 JP 769001](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769001); [攻略大百科 1833](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/1833)). Mew appears when 27 Mysterious Slates complete the Withered Wasteland mural. Articuno, Zapdos and Moltres come from altar kits in Palette Town, and meeting all three gives the Tidal Bell recipe for Lugia. Ho-Oh needs sun and the Clear Bell ([Game8 JP まっさらな街](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767586); [Altema](https://altema.jp/pocoapokemon/densetu)). Raikou, Entei, Suicune and Mewtwo are chance encounters on doll Dream Islands ([Serebii Dream Islands](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/dreamislands.shtml)). **Jirachi is the only legendary with a habitat** ([Bulbapedia Jirachi](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Jirachi_(Pok%C3%A9mon))), and it arrived only after its event ended ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/pokopia-jirachi-event-guide-habitat-wish-notes/)). Event Pokémon move in through event habitats and stay afterwards. Apart from Jirachi (data.json E005), legendaries belong in a planner's progression model, not its spawn model.

## Comfort is a six-step rank built from a short checklist

### Move-in needs a free, liked, large-enough home with three kinds of furniture

A move takes five steps. Build the new home, which need not be the habitat the Pokémon spawned from. Pick 「いっしょに行こう」 (come with me), walk until the nearby-habitat panel appears, pick 「ここにおひっこしする？」 (move here?), and the home changes. The move is refused if the destination already has a resident or **contains something the Pokémon dislikes**: Charmander will not move in next to water ([Game8 JP 767983](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767983)). A house needs **three different kinds of furniture** before anyone moves in, and three copies of one item do not count ([Game8 JP 767513](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767513); [攻略大百科 3509](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3509); [gemukan](https://www.gemukan.com/pokoa-building-001/)). Whether the rule keeps applying afterwards is contested. ぽこポケ学会 marks it "(引っ越し時のみ)" (only at move-in) [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)), while Game8's redecorating page warns that a house dropping below three stops being a house and evicts its residents [guide] ([Game8 JP 模様替え](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769142)). The safe planner behaviour is to warn whenever a populated house falls below three kinds.

Capacity is fixed by building type. **Den (おへや) and hut (こや) kits hold 1, cottage (いえ) kits 2, two-floor grand kits 4 (2 per floor), and any block house 4 whatever its size** ([Game8 JP 改築](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769081); [ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)); data.json's `cap` values match. A block house needs an enclosed ring at least 1 high with a door, a floor of at least 2×2 (1×4 fails) and smaller than 9×10, and no block directly in front of the door ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). Size limits also apply. "Small Pokémon can live in big houses, but big ones such as Charizard and Suicune can't live in small houses" ([攻略大百科 3509](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3509)), and "Pokémon can't use a bed or chair smaller than themselves" (same source). ぽこポケ学会 found Spinarak, Wooper, Glimmet, Glimmora, Swablu, Snivy, Combee and Vileplume happy in the smallest kit, while **Amaura**, Haxorus, Clodsire, Cacturne, Blissey and Venusaur need a hut or larger [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 ちらかす](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/)). Kit houses can be remodelled only into a larger kit, furniture carries over, and nobody may be inside ([Game8 JP 改築](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769081)). Since Ver. 1.1.0 a resident's floor in a two-storey house can be chosen from inside [official] ([Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)). Ditto's own house accepts residents too, which clears Palette Town's "ポケモンと住もう" challenge ([Game8 JP 769154](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769154)). Fan "roommate calculators" score housemates by shared ideal habitat and favourites, but the most careful of them says it does not verify any in-game roommate rule ([pokopiamap](https://pokopiamap.com/roommate-matchmaker)). The only housemate interaction a source supports is that one item in overlapping ranges can serve several homes ([Game8 JP 767983](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767983)).

### Six comfort ranks come from rest, play, decoration, food and environment

| Rank (JP) | Serebii EN | Environment points | Points if legendary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 最高 | Awesome | **10** | 20 |
| めちゃイイ感じ | Great | **5** | 10 |
| ちょっといい感じ | Nice | **2** | 4 |
| ふつう | Average | **1** | 2 |
| びみょう | Iffy | 0 | 0 |
| すみかなし (no home) | "Comfy Level 0" | 0 | 0 |

Sources: [Serebii Environment & Comfy Level](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/environmentlevel.shtml); [ぼってぃ 環境レベルを検証する](https://note.com/bottical/n/nee0a7bbcf440); [PokoTraqr calculator](https://www.pokotraqr.com/paradise/env-calculator) (search summary).

ぽこポケ学会 ranks the inputs roughly by importance. First comes **no disliked thing in range** (fire near a Grass type, water near a Fire type); then a liked environment (the `ideal` field: Bright, Dark, Warm, Cool, Humid or Dry); then not being hungry; then **a rest item, a toy and a decoration all in range**; then some favourites in range, where "同じ家具は加算されない or 効果が著しく薄れる？" (the same furniture doesn't add, or adds much less?). Two weaker factors follow: kit house > block house > habitat (a hypothesis) and a road connected to the Pokémon Center ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). The in-game tips list the same plus factors and name disliked things and an unpaved path to the Pokémon Center as minus factors ([ぼってぃ](https://note.com/bottical/n/nee0a7bbcf440)). ぼってぃ proposes a score in which rest and favourite-flavour food each give 0–2, toys and decorations together 0–6, and a liked environment 0 or 2. A total of **9+ gives Awesome, 6–8 Great, 4–5 Nice and 0–3 Average**. Their worked Lotad example reaches 10, but the tester calls the mapping "purely a feel". Two of their observations are firmer: favourite food alone for seven days never got past Nice, and non-favourite items alone also cap at Nice ([ぼってぃ](https://note.com/bottical/n/nee0a7bbcf440)). Favourites matter for speed as well as rank: a matching item raises comfort "at a higher rate" and shows **a bigger sparkle** when placed ([Serebii Favorites](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/favorites.shtml)).

Three behaviours make comfort a state rather than a formula. **Comfort changes only when the Pokémon notices**: it says 「見てみるよ」 then 「ありがとう」, off-screen Pokémon notice nothing, and leading a Pokémon to the item or reloading the town triggers the check ([ぼってぃ](https://note.com/bottical/n/nee0a7bbcf440); [ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). **Comfort falls reluctantly.** In one test, moving a Pokémon to a bright spot, blocking its path to the Pokémon Center, lighting a dark-lover's room and walling a home in ice all failed to drop it below Average. Yet a fresh resident starts at Iffy, and a home assigned from homeless reached Awesome in one day with every condition met ([ぼってぃ](https://note.com/bottical/n/nee0a7bbcf440)). Removing furniture does make Pokémon sad, and since Ver. 2.0.0 they "notice more quickly when the same furniture is replaced" [official] ([Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)). Third, **a house insulates its residents from the outdoors**: "a Pokémon living in a house is no longer affected by outdoor environment changes", and the bigger the house, the easier comfort rises, with the shop's grand (りっぱな) kits easiest of all ([Game8 JP 767983](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767983); [Game8 JP 767513](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767513)). Comfort can be read for every resident at the Pokémon Center PC (the − button), or asked with 「すみごこち どう？」, which reveals **one wish per Pokémon per day**. Fulfilling it lifts comfort one rank, and a hungry Pokémon always asks for food first ([Game8 JP 767513](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767513); [攻略大百科 3509](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3509)). Ditto's own house has a comfort rating that counts toward the environment level. It maxes at **13 kinds of furniture**, and only 5 kinds each of toy, rest and decoration are evaluated [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)).

For a planner this splits cleanly. A layout can be checked statically for the trio, favourites in range, the liked environment, no disliked things and house type, and from those it can predict a **ceiling rank**. The rank a Pokémon actually holds depends on noticing, daily wishes and feeding, which no layout can show, so the planner should present the ceiling and say so.

### Food, friendship and requests feed comfort on daily limits

Food has five flavours plus plain: sweet, spicy, bitter, dry and sour. **No Pokémon has plain as its favourite**, so plain foods (Leppa Berry, plain salad, fresh water) are the ones to spend freely ([Game8 JP 771031](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/771031); [Serebii flavours](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/flavors.shtml)). Favourite-flavour food raises comfort and friendship more than other food, and cooked dishes more than raw. Food left on a plate near the habitat, or berries on nearby trees, gets eaten automatically ([GameWith 545675](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545675); [Game8 JP 767513](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767513)). Feeding a plain berry first can use up the day's meal slot so the favourite food does not count ([ぼってぃ](https://note.com/bottical/n/nee0a7bbcf440)).

Friendship is a separate, **hidden** meter. It shows only as the Pokémon calling you by name instead of "Ditto", giving material gifts and getting a heart mark in the Pokédex at maximum, and it has not been seen to fall ([攻略大百科 4899](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4899); [Serebii Friendship](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/friendship.shtml)). A gift raises it **once per day per Pokémon**, and reaching maximum takes 6 to 8 favourite-flavour berries depending on comfort. Extra gains come from accepting play invitations, talking to a Pokémon with a 「・・・」 bubble, leading it, cooking or building with it, and since Ver. 2.0.0 from a following Scrub Pokémon cleaning it ([攻略大百科 4899](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4899); [Serebii Friendship](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/friendship.shtml)). Serebii says winning a game gives more than losing, and 攻略大百科's tests say it makes no difference. Friendship raises the chance of being taught an emote, and it reaches the environment level only through the comfort that friendship-raising actions also give ([攻略大百科 4899](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4899)), so the planner can leave it out.

Requests come in two kinds. **Important requests** are story steps: Rebuild the Pokémon Center, Yawn Up a Storm, Brighten Things Up, Time to Party, Rebuild the Huge Building, Build the Beautiful Building and the Team Initiation Challenge. **General requests** are side tasks such as "Build a house and put three different pieces of furniture inside" ([Game8 EN requests](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/583451)). Rewards are moves for Ditto, recipes and, most often, comfort for the requester. Each environment level unlocks new requests from the town's residents ([Game8 JP 767551](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767551)). Ver. 2.0.0 fixed requests that "may occasionally ask for items that the requesting Pokémon actually dislikes" [official] ([Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)). Comfort also drives two specialties: **Litter drops more items at higher comfort** ([Serebii Litter](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/litter.shtml)), and Crush gives rare white and black paint "when in good condition" ([攻略大百科 2257](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/2257)).

## Environment level turns comfort into a 160-point ladder per town

### Points sum per town, legendaries count double, and the level lags on the way down

"環境レベルは街に住んでいるポケモン達の「すみごこち」の合計で決まります" (the environment level is set by the total comfort of the Pokémon living in the town) ([Game8 JP 767551](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767551)). ぼってぃ solved the point values from two towns whose meters matched: Rocky Ridges with 4 Awesome, 5 Great, 13 Nice and 2 Average, and Sparkling Skylands with 2, 8, 15 and 3, both totalling 93 under **10 / 5 / 2 / 1**. The tester then reset Rocky Ridges to level 1 and rebuilt it to measure the thresholds [community-tested] ([ぼってぃ](https://note.com/bottical/n/nee0a7bbcf440)). Reddit user u/mogsetsu reached the same values independently, adding that **legendary and mythical residents score double** and that Ditto scores like a regular Pokémon. Their figures are known through PokoTraqr's calculator and an X translation linked from ぽこポケ学会 [community-tested, read via search summary] ([PokoTraqr](https://www.pokotraqr.com/paradise/env-calculator); [あみにし on X](https://x.com/aminisi/status/2034427479033827747)).

| Level | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative points | 3 | 12 | 26 | 52 | 77 | 100 | 120 | 140 | **160** |

So **16 Awesome regular residents, or 8 Awesome legendaries, reach level 10**, as does a mix such as 8 Awesome plus 16 Great. Level 2's value of 3 is the shakiest, read from a meter about a third full after one Average resident. **The level keeps a margin when points fall.** Level 5 survived at about 50 points after dropping below 52, and PokoTraqr says a level drops only below about 75 % of its band ([ぼってぃ](https://note.com/bottical/n/nee0a7bbcf440); [PokoTraqr](https://www.pokotraqr.com/paradise/env-calculator)). A third calculator uses a 0–8 point scale divided by 8 with habitat-variety and legendary bonuses, but cites no test and contradicts both measured sources ([Pokopia Island calculator](https://pokopiaisland.com/tools/environment-calculator)). Guides also list requests, rebuilding the Pokémon Center, paving the road and building kits as ways to raise the level ([Game8 JP 767551](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767551); [GameWith 545345](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545345)). Under the tested model these most likely work through residents' comfort rather than as flat bonuses. Serebii's line that kits help "even if a Pokémon doesn't immediately live there" is the only claim of a non-resident source, and nobody has tested it ([Serebii](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/environmentlevel.shtml)). The level falls when a resident leaves for another town (more for a happier one), when a habitat or nearby furniture is removed, when a comfort item stays broken, and when rain puts out an unroofed fire ([GameWith 545345](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545345); [Game8 JP 767551](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767551)). A drop **re-locks the unpurchased shop stock and the challenges of that level** ([Serebii](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/environmentlevel.shtml)).

The planner should compute score = Σ resident points (×2 for legendaries), counting Ditto's house as a resident, and level = the highest L with score ≥ T[L]. It should show the lag as "held until roughly three-quarters of the way back into the band", and warn when a planned move would drop a town below a gate level. Both tests date from Ver. 1.0.x (March 2026). Nobody has re-measured since 1.1.0 or 2.0.0, and whether story residents such as Peakychu or Chef Dente count is unconfirmed.

### Every town has its own ten-level ladder with a fixed kit pattern

Each town, including Palette Town, Bubbly Basin and each Cloud Island, has its own ladder adding shop stock, PC challenges and requests. Level 5 brings a gift and level 10 recipes ([Serebii](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/environmentlevel.shtml)). Serebii's lists count, for example, 28 items and 37 recipes in Withered Wasteland and 159 items on Cloud Island, and GameWith's lists (updated 23 Nov 2026) start at level 1 and include recipe sets that Serebii omits ([GameWith 545345](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545345)). The story towns share a pattern.

| 4 | Cottage (いえ) kit |
| 4–5 | Coloured hut kit: Pink in Withered Wasteland and Orange in Bleak Beach at 5; Gray in Rocky Ridges and Yellow in Sparkling Skylands at 4 per GameWith, 5 per Serebii |

The two lists disagree on about fifteen placements. PP Up is at Withered Wasteland level 4 per Serebii but level 3 in all four towns per GameWith. Serebii puts the Gray and Yellow huts at level 5, the Fountain plaza at Palette Town level 8 against GameWith's 4, the Safe at Palette Town level 6 against 7, and the Wasteland Pokémon Center kit under Bleak Beach ([GameWith 545345](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545345); [Serebii](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/environmentlevel.shtml)). GameWith is newer and agrees with data.json on several entries Serebii lacks, so it is the better tiebreaker. The planner should treat shop unlocks as "available while level ≥ N" and keep a **purchase source separate from the recipe source**. Many items are sold well before their recipe unlocks: the Garden bench is in stock at Withered Wasteland level 2, but its recipe comes at level 7.

### Story gates follow Trainer Rank, while levels 3 and 5 gate the Pokémon Center, the credits and Bubbly Basin

Towns open by **Trainer Rank**, not environment level. Super comes from Withered Wasteland's "Yawn Up a Storm" and opens Rocky Ridges and Bleak Beach, in either order. Hyper comes from clearing both "Time to Party" and "Brighten Things Up" and opens Sparkling Skylands. Master comes from "Rebuild the Huge Building" and opens the Team Initiation Challenge back in the Wasteland ([Game8 JP トレーナーランク](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768203); [Serebii Important Requests](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/importantrequests.shtml)). Environment level gates three things. Level 3 in each town starts its Pokémon Center rebuild ([Game8 JP story chart](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767282)). **Level 5 in all four story towns is needed for the credits**, because Team Initiation Challenge stage 7 wants the Washing Machine, Refrigerator and Game Boy and stage 9 two Party Poppers ([Serebii Team Initiation Challenge](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/teaminitiationchallenge.shtml)). **Bleak Beach level 5 unlocks Dive and, with the Expansion Pass, Bubbly Basin** ([Game8 JP 803611](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/803611)). Bubbly Basin's own level 3 starts its temple story ([Serebii Important Requests](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/importantrequests.shtml)). The post-game adds Magnet Rise, Mew, the full-Pokédex developer room and the legendary-bird kits, and Game8 counts 300 Pokédex entries, 219 habitats, 714 recipes and 1,195 items after Part 1 ([Game8 JP クリア後](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767901)).

**Palette Town is open from the start** according to Game8 JP, reached by bridging the broken road west of Withered Wasteland with blocks ([Game8 JP まっさらな街](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767586)). pokopia.center agrees it opens early, after Rock Smash, across 13+ bridge planks or by Surf, while pokopiahub and Fextralife call it post-game at Master rank and level 7 [search summaries] ([pokopia.center](https://pokopia.center/posts/pokopia-palette-town-guide-unlock-2026/); [pokopiahub](https://www.pokopiahub.com/guides/area-unlock-guide/)). The early reading has two independent sources, one read in full. In Palette Town, eight Special Challenges each award an Eeveelution, and several are planner-checkable counts: 12 flower kinds from seed (Vaporeon), **50 crops growing at once** (Leafeon), 10 residents (Sylveon), a Pokémon living in your house (Espeon), 15 buildings (Flareon), **300 road blocks** (Umbreon), **50 powered machines** (Jolteon) and 10 lift platforms (Glaceon) ([Game8 JP まっさらな街](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767586)).

There is no single town rating. GameWith's team found that the **sum of environment levels across towns raises the odds of a legendary on Dream Islands: 1 in 20 visits at a total of 10, and 10 in 20 at 50** ([GameWith 545345](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545345)). That is only two data points, and the test predates Bubbly Basin raising the possible total to 60. Restoring dry ground scores nothing directly. It matters because it spawns the first Pokémon, feeds Withered Wasteland's humidity story and builds habitats, which then feed comfort ([Game8 JP story chart](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767282)).

### Recipes arrive by seven routes, and Pokédex milestones are mostly settled

Recipes come from the town shop ladders, Pokédex milestones, story and requests, first-time pickups ("Create Iron ingot for first time"), daily shop specials and sparkling ripples, relic appraisal and trades, and events ([Serebii Crafting](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/crafting.shtml)). Main Pokédex rewards come every 5 registrations up to 100, then every 10 up to 300, and the Basin Pokédex has its own milestones at 5, 10, 15, 25, 35 and 50 or 52 ([Serebii Pokédex Completion](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/pokedexcompletion.shtml); [Game8 JP 767402](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767402)). data.json's `s` field mirrors Serebii's crafting page almost string for string, so it inherits that page's gaps (see the last section).

## Specialties fall into four trigger classes, and nine run unattended

### Most specialties wait for Ditto to ask

data.json lists 33 specialty labels, including "???" and Ditto's Transform. Of its 367 Pokémon, **168 have two specialties**, and a Pokémon with two uses both ([Serebii Specialty](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/specialty.shtml)). Every specialty fires in one of four ways ([攻略大百科 2257](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/2257)).

| Class | Specialties | What a planner must model |
|---|---|---|
| **Runs from home, unattended** | Litter; Gather; Grow living on the farm; Water with a filled basin; Generate "in their free time"; Burn, Chop, Recycle and Crush processing at a nearby community box or furnace | Placement of home, box, furnace, basin and field; display cap for Gather |
| Acts when a follower is led to a target | Burn (lights fires), Grow, Water, Generate | Nothing spatial beyond the target |
| Talk-to processing or service | Chop, Crush, Burn, Recycle, Rarify, Scrub, Engineer, Paint, Appraise, Collect, Gather Honey, Fly, Teleport, Storage, DJ, Yawn, Eat, Trade, Dream Island, Explode | Inventory and recipes only |
| Leads a project | Build, Bulldoze, Engineer, Party | Headcounts per kit |

Hard numbers are few. **Grow** advances a watered plant one stage, once per plant per day, and cannot move an unwatered crop. A flower needs only one stage, so it finishes at once ([Serebii Vegetables](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/vegetables.shtml); [Game8 EN Grow](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585561); [攻略大百科 2257](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/2257)). **Generate** power lasts until the next 05:00 ([攻略大百科 4588](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4588)). **Gather Honey** costs 3 honey per trade, 4 trades a day ([攻略大百科 2257](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/2257)). **DJ Rotom** holds 5 CDs and loops them ([Serebii CDs](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/cds.shtml)). **Water, Grow and Gather Pokémon can get hungry mid-job** and stop until fed ([Game8 EN 582704](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/582704)). Pokémon processing at a community box or furnace prefer the one nearest their own habitat since Ver. 1.1.0, and Ver. 2.0.0 let Paint, Gather Honey and Collect repeat within one conversation and Build work underwater [official] ([Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)). **No source gives a tile range** for Grow, Water, Burn or Generate acting from home. Guides say only "near" or "on" the farm, machine or box ([Game8 JP 自動化](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769942)), so the planner should label any reach it draws as an estimate.

**Litter** is the best documented. Drops are keyed to the **home's position**, not the Pokémon's: a kit house's orthogonal 1-tile ring, or a built house's floor plus 1 tile outside its walls. They never land while Ditto can see the spot, need block ground, and skip tiles holding an object or liquid (an open door is fine) ([ぽこポケ学会 ちらかす](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/)). Each Litter Pokémon drops one fixed material, honey caps at 1 in stock, fossil Pokémon drop stone in bulk while Blissey drops little, and excess drops wait internally and re-drop when space frees ([Splatoonブキ研究所](https://note.com/splatoonweapon/n/n0c7d1da22923)). ぽこポケ学会's own figures of a check every 15 minutes and a stop at 5 uncollected items are marked unconfirmed by the site itself. **Gather** picks up only items it can see, walks to them, chooses the action rarely, ignores field crops and fallen berries, works only while displayed, and is "effectively unusable on Cloud Islands" ([Splatoonブキ研究所](https://note.com/splatoonweapon/n/n0c7d1da22923); [ぽこポケ学会 畑](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/harvest/)). Its box must sit within about 4 tiles of the home's dotted range horizontally and +10/−8 vertically, and only one box is used [unverified, search summary and an X post] ([nomanoma-web](https://nomanoma-web.com/2026/03/%E3%80%90%E3%81%BD%E3%81%93%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%80%91%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B1%E3%83%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%81%8C%E3%81%97%E3%82%8F%E3%81%91%E3%81%A6%E3%81%8F%E3%82%8C%E3%81%AA%E3%81%84%E5%8E%9F%E5%9B%A0%EF%BC%81/); [X @winter8636](https://x.com/winter8636/status/2037171863265353976)). Game8's working ratio is **3 or more Litter Pokémon to 1–2 Gather**, ideally with Pokémon holding both Gather and Burn: its example is トロッゴン (Carkol), and the Rolycoly line all carry Burn + Gather in data.json ([Game8 JP 自動化](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769942)). **Trade** opens a barter shop at a powered register on a table, with more goods on a wider counter, payment in up to 4 item kinds, **+50 % value for items the trader likes**, at most **10 registers trading at once per town**, and no way to force a shop open ([Serebii Trade](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/trade.shtml); [ぽこポケ学会 色々な上限](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)). A register powered only by a Generate Pokémon goes dead after the day, so guides recommend utility poles ([攻略大百科 4307](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4307)). Magikarp, Kyogre, Lugia and Ho-Oh have no specialty ("???"), and Magikarp alone can be given a home ([Serebii none list](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/pokedex/specialty/none.shtml)).

### Kits need a specialty headcount, a fixed time and a building-point budget

A kit starts once its materials and the Pokémon on its sign are gathered. **Only the specialties matter, not the pictured Pokémon**, and every house kit needs at least one Build Pokémon ([攻略大百科 1611](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/1611); [Game8 JP 767983](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767983)). Block houses need no sign, no Build Pokémon and no wait. Build time is a property of the kit: about **15 minutes** for dens, **1 hour** for huts and utilities, and **the next 05:00** for cottages, two-floor houses and landmarks ([Serebii Leaf hut kit](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/build/leafhutkit.shtml); [Serebii Building](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/building.shtml)). **Tinkmaster (Engineer) as leader turns a next-day build into one hour**, which matters most on Cloud Islands, where the clock cannot be moved ([攻略大百科 1611](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/1611)). Each town has **40 building points**, each kit costing 1 or 2, and at most **14 builds in progress** ([Serebii Building](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/building.shtml); [ぽこポケ学会 色々な上限](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)). Moving or demolishing a kit house costs no materials, takes 15 minutes per step, needs the original headcount with one Bulldoze Pokémon, refunds everything on demolition, and works only for kit houses, never block houses or habitats ([GameWith 547850](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547850)). Up to **5 Pokémon** can follow Ditto ([Game8 JP 768614](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768614)). Whether extra Build Pokémon shorten a kit, and whether a two-specialty Pokémon fills two slots, are both untested; the claims that they do are search snippets.

### Pokémon live on a short leash, sleep at night and follow roads

Most Pokémon sleep at night. Kit-house residents go indoors to sleep, others use Relaxation furniture or sofa edges, and talking to a sleeper wakes it instantly with no penalty, so night reduces activity rather than locking work ([Game8 JP 769153](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769153); [Game8 EN 585501](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585501); [ぽこポケ学会 ちらかす](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/chirakasu/)). Nocturnal Pokémon sleep at home by day, and while Peakychu glows (Illuminate), Pokémon "stay lively even at night" ([Serebii Time & Day](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/timeday.shtml); [攻略大百科 2257](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/2257)). The AI repertoire is small: idle near home, visit friends or Ditto, use a hot spring, walk to a register shop. Pokémon have **a maximum action range**, so a behaviour too far away never happens [community-tested] ([Splatoonブキ研究所, 2 Apr](https://note.com/splatoonweapon/n/ncb41c8dfc076)). In Rocky Ridges the fossil Pokémon in the mine museum almost never come out, and on Cloud Islands a south-east Pokémon Center leaves about three-quarters of the island a dead zone. The tester's advice is to put the Pokémon Center, hot spring and player house at the map centre and ring the homes around them. Roads let Pokémon travel farther ([Game8 EN 585501](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585501)), but a road of wall-art blocks is not recognised as a road ([ぽこポケ学会 その他](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/others/)). Followers separate where a path is too narrow for their body, while small flyers rarely do ([Game8 JP 768614](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768614)). A Pokémon touching magma respawns at the Pokémon Center and walks the shortest route home ([ぽこポケ学会 クロック回路](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/circuit/clock/)). 95 Pokémon can go underwater, 9 of them only walking the floor ([Serebii Dive](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/dive.shtml)). For a planner, the useful outputs are distance-to-centre warnings and a road-connectivity check, not a wander radius, since no source gives one in tiles.

## Crafting is instant at the bench, timed at the furnace, and paid for in challenge coins

### The workbench reads four adjacent boxes, and blocks come ten at a time

Workbench crafting has no timer: you choose a quantity and get the items, and block recipes make **10 per craft** ([Serebii Crafting](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/crafting.shtml); [Game8 JP ブロック](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768702)). The bench draws on the bag plus **up to 4 orthogonally adjacent storage boxes**; diagonal, stacked and fifth boxes do not count [community-tested] ([X @kk6](https://x.com/kk6/status/2033939774445391899); [GameWith 547349](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547349)). Since Ver. 2.0.0 "materials in the storage box next to the workbench will be consumed before materials in the bag" [official] ([Serebii patch notes](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)). A shared storage device (portal pod) beside the bench is used first, and following Gulpin or Swalot can be crafted from ([Game8 803649](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/803649); [GameWith 547349](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547349)). Capacities: the bag starts at 20 slots and grows to 120 with four Packing Tips books plus a 10-slot Handy Bag; stacks hold 99; a storage box holds 20 slots, a big box 60 and a portal pod 80 shared across every main town ([Game8 bag](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767463); [GameWith 547349](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547349); [Game8 803649](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/803649)). A preview figure of 140 pod slots and a Gulpin capacity of 20 or 30 remain contested. Per-town container caps are 256 storage-type furniture, 256 big boxes, 32 community boxes, 32 baskets and 127 plates [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 色々な上限](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)).

### Furnaces are 1:1 and real-time, and concrete is the one disputed ratio

| Process | Station and helper | Ratio | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iron, copper, gold ore → ingot; Pokémetal and Rare Pokémetal fragments → metal; beach sand, sandstone or volcanic ash → glass | Smelting furnace + Burn; one material type at a time | **1:1** | 4 min per item (99 = 6 h 36 min) or 2 min, by two testers |
| Same inputs | Metallic smelting furnace + Burn | 1:1 | **exactly half** the normal time |
| Limestone → concrete | Concrete mixer + one Crush Pokémon, hose in water | **1:4** per GameWith; 1:1 in data.json | about 1 hour (unverified) |
| Small log → lumber | Chop | 1:5 | — |
| Squishy clay → brick | Burn | 1:2 | — |
| Nonburnable garbage → iron ore; wastepaper → paper | Recycle | 1:2 | — |
| Iron ingot → Tinkagear | Tinkmaster | — | — |
| Star Piece → Rare Pokémetal (Star Piece = 99 Stardust) | Porygon-Z (Rarify) | 1:1 | real-time wait |

Sources: [GameWith furnace](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547415); [Game8 EN furnace](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/586265); [X @nominz](https://x.com/nominz/status/2033745188921938309); [GameWith metallic furnace](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/549467); [GameWith concrete](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547572); [Game8 JP ざいもく](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767690); [Game8 JP 自動化](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769942); [Bulbapedia Rarify](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Rarify_(specialty)).

Which Burn Pokémon runs a furnace makes no difference to speed: five Pokémon from different towns and comfort levels timed the same ([hyperts.net](https://hyperts.net/pokopoke/moyasu/)). No fuel is consumed, and moving the console clock forward finishes processing at once ([GameWith concrete](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547572)). A furnace placed in a Burn Pokémon's home smelts whenever that Pokémon is in ([Game8 EN furnace](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/586265)). GameWith repeats **1 limestone → 4 concrete** on two pages updated in November 2026 ([GameWith 545349](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545349)), which makes it the stronger reading against data.json's 1:1.

### Renewables tick hourly or daily, and town ore runs out

Berry trees grow **1 berry per hour, up to 3**, even underwater, and stop while withered. Fallen berries land on 6 fixed spots and vanish on area change [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 木](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/tree/); [GameWith berries](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/548099)). **Pokémetal ore in the towns is finite**, after which Dream Islands are the only source ([GameWith 545349](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545349)), and no source measures a respawn timer for any town rock or deposit. Glowing dig spots and ripples move every 05:00, a powered vending machine gives one drink a day, and every Sunday is a Lucky Day with more ripples and dropped items ([GameWith daily](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545468); [Game8 JP ラッキーデー](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768278)). Up to 512 dropped items persist per town, oldest first out ([ぽこポケ学会 色々な上限](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)).

### Coins come only from challenges and stamps, and trade is a separate points economy

**Life Coins come only from PC challenges and the weekly stamp card**: "チャレンジのクリア以外ではコインを入手できない" (coins can only be earned by clearing challenges) ([GameWith money](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547206); [Game8 JP 767358](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767358)). English wikis claiming surplus can be sold at the PC give no price list, and two maintained Japanese guides say no such option exists. Daily challenges reset at 05:00 and pay 300 on Fridays (+50 %) ([Serebii Time & Day](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/timeday.shtml)). The stamp card gives one stamp a day and pays on Fridays; Serebii and GameWith disagree on the values ([Serebii stamp card](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/stampcard.shtml); [GameWith 547795](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547795)). The shop's "today only" stock refreshes at 05:00 and grows with environment level ([GameWith shop](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547145)). Known prices include a workbench recipe for 50, a Pokémon Center rebuild kit for 1,000, a relocation kit for 10, the Pokémetal 10-pack for 50 at level 6, the metallic furnace recipe for 500 and the portal pod recipe for 3,000 ([GameWith 545330](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545330); [GameWith Pokémetal](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547936); [Game8 803649](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/803649)). Trade runs on item point values instead: Pokémetal ×5 costs 1,000 points at the PC trade, and liked items count 1.5× ([GameWith Pokémetal](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547936); [Serebii Trade](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/trade.shtml)).

Cooking uses five tools, each with a fixed main ingredient: cutting board (salads, leaf), pot (soups, fresh water), frying pan (hamburger steaks, bean), bread oven (breads, wheat) and, with the Expansion Pass, the mixer (smoothies, watermelon slice). The pot and pan need a campfire or stove, and a helper's specialty can change the dish ([GameWith cooking](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545733); [Serebii Cooking](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/cooking.shtml)). **The Mosslax offering** is the one daily buff that touches planning. Its dominant flavour picks the effect: spicy brings more habitat spawns, sweet more relics, bitter more ripples and honey, dry more Ho-Oh, Lugia and rare feathers, and sour better shop stock. It works once a day and lasts until 05:00 ([Serebii Mosslax boosts](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/mosslaxboosts.shtml)). Paint comes from giving berries to a Crush Pokémon at 1 berry to 5 paint, **with white and black only as random bonuses**. Its 18 colours include 10 two-paint mixes, and Smeargle applies them only to items with a brush icon, one paint mark per paintable part ([Serebii paint](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/paint.shtml); [official how-to-play](https://www.pocoapokemon.jp/ja/howtoplay/260522_02/)).

## Ditto's moves have two states each, and every timer reads the console clock

### Fifteen learned moves, no move levels, and food as the only power-up

So a planner needs two states per move, normal and fed.

Water Gun revives withered grass, trees and flowers, moistens dry soil for tilling, puts out fires and hardens lava, but washes mud only after a Water-specialty Pokémon has bubbled it ([Game8 JP みずでっぽう](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768019); [GameWith みずでっぽう](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547665)). Since Ver. 2.0.0, Leafage on farm soil turns it back into normal soil [official] ([Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)). On foot Ditto reaches the tile in front, or the block above or below by aiming. **Mouse mode reaches about 6 tiles but works only for Rock Smash** ([攻略大百科 建築テクニック](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/5901); [Game8 JP マウス操作](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769074)). There is no terraform brush and **no blueprint tool**. PP is Ditto's only resource. A Leppa Berry restores about 25 %, a bean 35 % and a fresh carrot 75 %, any cooked meal refills fully, the Pokémon Center refills once a day, and Point Up from a level-3 shop raises the maximum ([Game8 JP PP回復](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767305)). No health, hunger, stamina or fall damage exists. Base PP and per-move costs are unpublished, so an effort estimate would be guesswork.

### Four time bands, a 05:00 reset, and one disputed hour

Pokopia runs 1:1 on the Switch 2 clock. Serebii and Game8 agree that evening is 17:00–19:00 and night 19:00–05:00, and **disagree only on 07:00–08:00**: Serebii puts it in Day (Morning 05:00–07:00), and Game8 JP in Morning (05:00–07:59) ([Serebii Time & Day](https://serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/timeday.shtml); [Game8 JP 769153](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769153)). GameWith also starts night at 19:00 ([GameWith 夜](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/548015)). The majority Japanese reading (05/08/17/19) should be the default, with Serebii's as a setting. No source mentions seasons, months or calendar weather. Friday brings the stamp payout and +50 % daily-challenge coins, and Sunday is Lucky Day.

**At 05:00 the in-game date changes.** Crops advance a stage if wet. The daily challenge, the shop's today-only stock and sometimes kit stock refresh. One Dream Island visit, one Pokémon Center refill, one Mosslax offering and a stamp become available again. Glowing spots move, waiting habitat Pokémon arrive and next-day kits finish ([Game8 JP 767333](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767333); [Game8 JP デイリー](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767308); [攻略大百科 毎日やること](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/851)). Berries, Litter, kit timers and random sparkles tick continuously instead. **Moving the clock forward has no confirmed penalty**: it completes builds, brings waiting Pokémon and runs every skipped reset, and the single autosave means there is nothing to roll back ([Game8 JP 767333](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767333); [Game8 JP 767517](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767517)). The one exception is events. **From Wish Upon a Jirachi (23 Jun 2026) onward, events check an online date**, so they can no longer be started or replayed by time travel ([OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/news/33202/pokemon-pokopias-popular-time-traveling-loophole-appears-to-have-been-patched)). Cloud Islands are not affected by date changes ([攻略大百科 毎日やること](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/851)). For a planner, "day N" runs from 05:00 to 04:59. Builds and arrivals are timestamp-based and progress offline, while berry and Litter accrual offline is unproven.

## Cloud Islands, Dream Islands and Bubbly Basin each break a main-world rule

*Most of this section rests on search summaries, because Nintendo's support pages and several guide hosts could not be opened; treat its details as guide-level at best.*

### Cloud Islands are separate save worlds that share only recipes

A Cloud Island is an online world for **up to 4 players at once**. It needs Nintendo Switch Online and opens with Link Play at Withered Wasteland level 2 ([Game8 EN Cloud Island](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585114); [Nintendo support 71349](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/71349/kw/pokopia)). It has a random layout from a shareable "Magic Number" seed, no facilities at the start, and only one active island per player; rerolling deletes the old one ([TheGamesWiki](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/cloud-island-guide); [Game8 EN map](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584822)). **It has its own bag, its own environment level (1–10) and its own 159-item shop.** Items and Pokémon never cross to the main world; recipes, and probably Pokédex progress, do ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/pokemon-pokopia-cloud-islands-guide/); [Serebii Cloud Islands](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/cloudislands.shtml); [Serebii](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/environmentlevel.shtml)). Its field-crop cap is **576** against 511 in a main town ([ぽこポケ学会 色々な上限](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)), and its portal pods share only with other pods on the same island ([攻略大百科 Ver.2.0.0](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/5548)). The official Ver. 2.0.0 note fixed "players other than the administrator" being able to place relocation kits, so an administrator tier exists ([pocoapokemon.jp news 136](https://www.pocoapokemon.jp/ja/news/136/)); whether guests can be stopped from building is unclear. The planner should treat a Cloud Island as its own area with its own ladder and caps, and should not let main-world inventory feed it.

### Dream Islands are daily material farms with legendary odds

Drifloon is the only Pokémon with the Dream Island specialty. Once it lives in its habitat ("Riding Warm Updrafts", three campfires under a roof), showing it a doll opens a temporary island **once a day**, and the island resets at 05:00, losing anything left behind ([Bulbapedia Dream Island](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Dream_Island_(specialty)); [GameWith 屋根](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/548162); [Nintendo support 71382](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/71382/~/how-to-visit-a-dream-island-(pok%C3%A9mon-pokopia)); [Serebii Dream Islands](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/dreamislands.shtml)). Each doll sets the island's materials: Pikachu gives twine, sea glass and seashells; Eevee Leppa Berries, vine rope and glowing mushrooms; Clefairy cave mushrooms, copper ore and **bulk limestone**; Arcanine iron ore, gold ore and glowing stone; Dragonite wastepaper, Pokémetal and crystal fragments; Starmie (Expansion Pass) sea grapes, coral and grubby pearls; Ditto and Substitute a random island ([Serebii Dream Islands](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/dreamislands.shtml); [GameWith concrete](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547572)). Each island also holds one Castform charm and yellow Poké Balls with Pokémetal, Rare Pokémetal or habitat hints ([GameWith 545349](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545349); [GameWith Pokémetal](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/547936)). Sources conflict on whether a trip uses up the doll ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/pokemon-pokopia-dream-island-guide-all-materials-dolls-and-islands)) and on the visit limit (one a day, or two with a Cloud Island) ([Rankedboost](https://rankedboost.com/pokopia/guides/dream-islands/)). The Starmie doll comes from Frillish's request at Bubbly Basin level 3 and leads to an island where Phione is the only Pokémon ([Game8 EN Phione](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/614446)). A planner should model Dream Islands as a daily yield table, not a plot.

### Bubbly Basin is paid, underwater and gated twice, and Palette Town is the only shared build site

Bubbly Basin is Expansion Pass Part 1, released with the free Ver. 2.0.0 on 4–5 Aug 2026. The **$34.99 pass covers all three parts** ([TPCi press release](https://press.pokemon.com/en/releases/Pokemon-Pokopia-Expansion-PassPart-1-Bubbly-Basin-Available-Now); [Game8 EN Expansion Pass](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/582236)). **Dive, underwater building and portal pods are free**, while the town, its story, watermelons, smoothies, buoyant blocks and the Starmie island need the pass ([TPCi press release](https://press.pokemon.com/en/releases/Pokemon-Pokopia-Expansion-PassPart-1-Bubbly-Basin-Available-Now)). Players without the pass can still build underwater in the base towns. Whirlpools spawn **7 to 20 tiles below the surface**, up to 5 at a time, regenerate during the day, and drop watermelons, seeds, star-spotted rock, Pokémetal and sea glass ([Serebii Whirlpools](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/whirlpools.shtml)). The deep sea goes dark and needs light, and buoyant blocks can be placed in water without support ([Serebii Dive](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/dive.shtml)). Part 2, "Dazzling Decorations", arrives on **9 Dec 2026** with free Ver. 3.0.0. It needs Withered Wasteland's level-5 and Pokémon Center requests and Drifloon's doll request, and adds accessories, furniture and new Pokémon ([pokemon.com Part 2](https://www.pokemon.com/us/news/pokemon-pokopia-expansion-pass-part-2-dazzling-decorations-available-december-9)). Every furniture and stacking rule should be re-checked once it lands.

Multiplayer guests can build only in **Palette Town**; every other town is visit-only, and guests cannot take Pokémon or items home ([Game8 JP マルチプレイ](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767437); [Game8 JP まっさらな街](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/767586)). GameShare works only there, for 2 players. The Mysterious Goggles (100 coins at level 3) open a view-only "Virtual Mode" tour of other islands, and builds are shared by photographing objects and 3D-printing them, not by any blueprint file ([Nintendo whatsnew](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/heres-how-multiplayer-works-in-pokemon-pokopia/); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/pokemon/pokemon-pokopia-developer-island/)). From Ver. 2.0.1, players must run the same version to play together ([Nintendo Life 2.0.1](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2026/09/pokemon-pokopia-updated-to-version-2-0-1-here-are-the-full-patch-notes)). No player-to-player trading, Pokémon HOME link or amiibo support turned up, so a planner export format would be its own feature, not a game one.

## The earlier reports' open questions mostly stay open

The ぽこポケ学会 site's source was last changed on 24 Jul 2026 ([github.com/kyu49/pokopia](https://github.com/kyu49/pokopia)), and ccopedia's newest test is the 14 Oct humidity article ([ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)). The height report missed three official lines, though. Since Ver. 2.0.0, items can be placed on top of 「しかくいうえ木」 (square hedges; Serebii's "square plants"), and doors and windows can be fine-tuned in depth. Since Ver. 1.1.0, a resident's floor in a two-storey house can be chosen from inside ([攻略大百科 Ver.2.0.0](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/5548); [Serebii patch notes](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/patch.shtml)).

Of the height report's thirteen questions, **one moved**. Question 5 (can items sit on fences, cube hedges, counters and shelves) is now **partly answered by the square-hedge line**, assuming しかくいうえ木 is the report's "cube hedge"; the planner's `acceptsOnTop` flag can be set true for it, labelled official since 2.0.0. The depth fine-tuning bears on question 6 (which cell a wall mount scores in) and question 12 (doors shifting the box), and the floor picker confirms that floors are separate assignments for question 8, but none of these settles a scoring rule. The other questions — furniture at h = +4, wall-top items, a roof at h = +1, items on picked-up tables, the tile outside the door, habitat recipe-part height tolerance, stacked houses sharing blocks, see-through roofs and raised interior floors — have no new test on any source reachable here. Most Japanese testers publish on X first, which this research could not open.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the planner's centre of gravity from geometry to population. The two earlier reports showed that tiles, heights and liquids reduce to static checks. This round shows that **everything that unlocks content flows through residents**: one per habitat, one per species, a comfort ceiling the layout sets, and a fixed point table that turns those ceilings into a town's level, which then opens the kits, recipes and story gates the player builds with. A planner can compute almost all of that from a layout. What it cannot see is the gap between the ceiling and the rank a Pokémon actually holds, which depends on noticing, daily wishes, feeding and the slow downward drift. The right product is a predicted ceiling and a predicted level, labelled as best case, plus warnings at the points that cost the player most: a town dropping below level 3 or 5, a house falling below three kinds, a Main habitat placed in Bubbly Basin, a habitat whose species are all met, more than about 21 visible residents in an automation town, and big Pokémon assigned to dens.

The weakest link is also the most valuable to fix. Every number the level model depends on comes from two fan tests from March 2026, before Ver. 1.1.0 and 2.0.0, and nobody has re-measured it since. A single afternoon of in-game tests would settle more than any further web research: re-solving the points table on a current save, pinning the 07:00–08:00 band, checking whether a house that drops below three kinds after move-in keeps its residents, timing a furnace and a concrete mixer, and stacking two Grow Pokémon on one field. Part 2 lands on 9 Dec 2026 with new furniture and Pokémon, so the data.json fixes above are best made now, before a new wave of items arrives to be reconciled.
