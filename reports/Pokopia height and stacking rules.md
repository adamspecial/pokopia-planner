# Door-level height bands decide what Pokopia counts

Pokémon Pokopia never asks what an item is resting on; it asks which block-sized cell the item occupies, counted in whole levels from the walking layer at the house door, and two windows built on that count do nearly all the work. The **recognition box**, the white dotted line the right stick draws, reaches **4 tiles past a house or habitat and from 4 levels below to 3 above the door**, and it decides furniture, favourites and the rest/toy/decoration trio, but only for items a Pokémon can walk to. The **environment band** of a block-built house is far tighter, covering only interior and door columns **from the floor-block layer (−1) to three above the walking layer (+3)**, so walls, wall tops and anything under a wall are ignored for light, warmth and humidity alike. Daylight falls only straight down, at **90 × unroofed floor-and-door tiles ÷ all house tiles including walls**; a roof block works at any height, and windows, time and weather do nothing. Kit interiors drop every height limit, while ten colour, log and office kits are really block houses that follow the block rules. Almost all of this is community-tested by two groups whose work was read in full, ccopedia's five note.com test articles (30 September to 14 October 2026, after Ver.2.0.0) and the ぽこポケ学会 wiki, because Nintendo has published no height rule at all. For the planner, a Z layer can score everything from one integer level per object relative to the door and needs support relations only for editing; most current assumptions survive, but the 3-furniture rule, the block-built kit list, habitat recipe tolerance and the placement of floor blocks and roofs in Z need correcting.

## Every count is a cell test measured from the door

*Brief item (3), plus the evidence base for every item.*

Labels follow the brief. **Confirmed** means official patch notes or in-game text, including in-game messages visible in testers' screenshots; **community-tested** means a player test that was read in full; **unverified** covers search snippets and guide pages that state a rule without showing a test, even where the guide page itself was read in full. No Nintendo or Pokémon Company page states a height or range rule, so the only official material is patch notes and item descriptions. The weightiest evidence is ccopedia's series on light ([完全版「あかるい」「くらい」](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f), which supersedes [「くらい」の判定検証](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4691e64139b0) and [明かりによる「あかるい」](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n489ac4eb3252)), warmth ([「あたたかい」「すずしい」](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df)) and humidity ([「うるおっている」「乾そうしている」](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)), alongside ぽこポケ学会's [家と住み心地](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/). The ccopedia articles postdate Ver.2.0.0 (4 August 2026) and match gamemos' April results, seen only as a snippet ([gamemos](https://gamemos.net/2026/04/10/post-140083/)), so no patch appears to have moved the bands.

Every height in this report uses ccopedia's convention: **h = 0 is the walking layer** inside the enclosure, the same layer as a 1-high wall ring and the door; **h = −1 is the floor block**; and +1, +2 and so on are the cells above the walking layer ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f)). In the planner, h is a cell's level minus the door tile's elevation, which is what the code already computes as `elev − base`.

### The recognition box reaches 4 tiles out and −4 to +3, but only where a Pokémon can walk

ccopedia found that a home's range extends **4 cells past the habitat or block house in all six directions**, and for habitats it confirmed the edge: items 4 above or below drew reactions, and items 5 away did not [community-tested] ([ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd); [ccopedia くらい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4691e64139b0)). For block houses, ぽこポケ学会 takes the door's height as 0 and recognises items up to 3 above, giving **"扉から-4マス～+3マスまでが認識範囲"**, the same range in which the top-left resident display appears [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). gamemos' Y = −3 to +4, measured from the floor block, is the same −4 to +3 from the walking layer, and a separate tester on X agrees, though both were seen only as snippets [unverified] ([gamemos](https://gamemos.net/2026/04/10/post-140083/); [あめしこうR2 on X](https://x.com/Ameshikou_R2/status/2040302712747950405)). The one-level dispute comes from ccopedia itself: a lamp at **+4** drew "あっ、○○のランプ～！" [confirmed, in-game text] and an air conditioner at +4 was noticed as furniture, although neither added any environment points [community-tested] ([ccopedia 明かり](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n489ac4eb3252); [ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df)).

Geometry is not the whole test. ぽこポケ学会 found that **"ポケモンがアクセス不可能だと、住心地に影響を与えない"** (items Pokémon cannot reach do not affect comfort), that furniture can be shared between houses, and that ground items stay recognised as long as the door is within the 5th cell above the ground [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). KYU adds that outside items stop counting when the height or the walk to them is too great [unverified, snippet] ([KYU on X](https://x.com/kyuphd/status/2030234005325668475)), and gamemos says a non-rectangular room's range is drawn around its bounding rectangle, 4 tiles past the walls [unverified] ([gamemos](https://gamemos.net/2026/04/10/post-140083/)). The dotted line only appears when the house has a resident, and it may not draw where the surrounding ground is at a very different height from the floor [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)).

### The environment band counts interior and door columns from −1 to +3

ccopedia splits a block house's surroundings into 床 (floor), defined as "ブロックとドアで囲まれた領域の内側の空間、およびドアが存在する空間" (the space inside the blocks and door, plus the door's own space), 壁 (any cell a house block occupies) and 屋外 (everything else), and it counts light, warmth and humidity only in **「床」とその上下-1～+3マス** [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f); [ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df); [ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)). The −1 layer is the floor itself. A vase sunk one deep counts for humidity, and so do the grass blocks that make up the indoor ground; magma poured one deep counts for warmth; anything **two deep is ignored**, so heat or water cannot be hidden under the floor [community-tested] ([ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd); [ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df)). Because floor blocks count, a block house of 30 floor tiles or fewer stays "dry" even with an all-grass floor, since grass is worth 1 per tile and dry means 30 or less [community-tested] ([ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)).

A Seedot lamp at +4 adds no light, while the door column counts at every height in the band: a hot rock on top of the door warmed the house, ice swapped in under the door cooled it, and water under the door added humidity [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f); [ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df); [ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)). Nothing outside counts, "家の外をいくら明るくしたり暗くしたりしても無意味" [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f)). ぽこポケ学会 reached the same band independently ("家の中とドアの上下のみ。床面-1～+3マス", marked 検証不十分, not fully verified) and guesses that the tile in front of the door counts too, which ccopedia's tests do not support; the band is community-tested and the door-front guess is untested ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)).

| Window | What it governs | Horizontal reach | Vertical reach (h) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recognition box (white dotted line) | Furniture, favourites, comfort trio, request furniture | 4 tiles past the house or habitat | −4 to +3; +4 disputed | Community-tested; only for items a Pokémon can reach |
| Block-house environment band | Light, warmth, humidity in block houses and block-built kits | Interior and door tiles only | −1 to +3 | Community-tested for all three |
| Habitat light and humidity | Light, humidity | Whole dotted box, no indoor or outdoor rule | −4 to +4 | Community-tested |
| Habitat warmth | Warmth | Whole dotted box | −1 to +3 | Community-tested |
| Fixed-kit interior | Light, warmth, humidity | Everything indoors | No limit | Community-tested |
| Community box (sorting) | Where a sorter's box works | 4 tiles past the habitat's dotted area | 10 above, 8 below the dotted area | Unverified |

Other mechanics also use 3D boxes, which supports modelling every range as a box rather than a flat ring. A floor switch affects a 5×5×5 cube centred on its tile, and a wall switch or card reader a 7×7×7 cube [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 回路](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/circuit/items/)), while dry rock reportedly acts only on the highest block from 1 above to 2 below it [unverified] ([aminisi on X](https://x.com/aminisi/status/2037509264559030467)).

## Tables, stands and block tops hold items, and nothing needs support once placed

*Brief item (1).*

Vertical placement moves in whole blocks. While placing, **ZL plus D-pad Up/Down shifts the item from 2 below to 2 above the targeted cell, five positions in all**, and the same control sets a water source's height [unverified, full-read guide] ([攻略大百科 建築テクニック集](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/5901)); Game8 describes the same D-pad height control [unverified] ([Game8 controls](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/582981)). No source mentions slabs or half-height blocks outdoors, and water is the only thing with fill levels below a whole block [unverified] ([Serebii water](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/water.shtml)). Inside kit houses, furniture moves in **half-tile steps** horizontally [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)), which Game8 repeats [unverified] ([Game8 EN houses](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)).

### Display furniture and block tops act as surfaces

The in-game text marks some furniture as display surfaces: the exhibition stand is "for displaying lots of different things on top", and the plain stand is "perfect for displaying items" [confirmed, in-game text seen via snippet] ([Bulbapedia items by category](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/List_of_items_by_category_in_Pok%C3%A9mon_Pokopia)). Game8 says of tables and large tables, "You may also place other small items on top of it to display them!" [unverified, guide] ([Game8 tables](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/586275); [Game8 large tables](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/586293)), and ぽこポケ学会 notes that the washing machine takes items on top, 「上にものを乗せることができる」 [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 回路](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/circuit/items/)). GameWith and 攻略大百科 stack big storage boxes two or more high with a block on top, use the wireless transmitter as a plinth for dolls, potted plants or flowers, and raise a utility pole one step by standing it on an iron pillar [unverified, full-read guides] ([GameWith 建築テクニック](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/548273); [攻略大百科](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/5901)).

ccopedia's tests show stacking directly: a thin candle sat on a block light and both counted, and a barrel and a desk sat on a 2-high block [community-tested] ([ccopedia 明かり](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n489ac4eb3252); [ccopedia くらい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4691e64139b0)). Its test houses also carried lamps, hot rocks and humidity items on their wall tops, which shows that ordinary items sit on top of wall blocks [community-tested] ([ccopedia 明かり](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n489ac4eb3252)). Snippets add a lamp on a low chest and a small rug on a square pillar used as a patterned table [unverified] ([blog](https://mugen-namakura.hatenablog.com/entry/2026/04/02/004745); [game-tier](https://game-tier.com/pokopoke/pocoa-architecture/)). The one documented refusal is floating weed, which follows the water level in its tile and takes no furniture, though dropped items rest on it [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 デザイン](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/design/)).

No source covers furniture on fences, cube hedges, counters or shelves, more than one small item per tile, an item resting on an item on a table, or what happens to items on a table when the table is picked up. The two guide claims about overlap are both untested and point in opposite directions: Game8 says furniture can go anywhere there is space, while pokopia.center says furniture cannot overlap furniture or blocks, which players contradict by combining partitions with desks into sofas [unverified] ([Game8 JP 模様替え](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769142); [pokopia.center](https://pokopia.center/posts/pokopia-building-guide-tips-2026/); [ナナセ on X](https://x.com/nanase__game/status/2032420006810775648)).

### Placed things keep their level when their support goes

Several guides independently describe building a temporary column, setting furniture on it and breaking the column, which leaves the item floating; The Games Wiki says this works for "most furniture", and players use sand as a quick-to-break support for furniture that will not stack [unverified, snippets] ([The Games Wiki](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/advanced-building-techniques); [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/pokemon-pokopia-pro-tips-tricks/); [game-tier](https://game-tier.com/pokopoke/pocoa-fur/)). Firmer evidence comes from ぽこポケ学会. You can break the block under a door after placing the door and the house still counts; the wooden-fence door needs ground under it when placed, but the gate door can be placed in mid-air; and the mirror ball needs a ceiling rather than ground, 「天井があれば、地面がなくても設置可能」 [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/); [ぽこポケ学会 回路](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/circuit/items/)). Rugs, flowers and shrubs normally need something beneath them at placement, though a workaround floats them [unverified] ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fP4MAnNBkVs)).

### Only doors and fixtures have measured heights

The measured heights all come from ぽこポケ学会's circuit page: the wooden-fence gate is 2 tiles, the big wooden door 2×2, the iron door 3 high by 2 wide and the shutter 3×3, while the ground-set lidded window counts as filling the cell above it and an open sash window has collision there [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 回路](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/circuit/items/)). A room you can walk into needs 3 blocks of headroom, with the 4th as the ceiling [unverified] ([Game8 EN houses](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)). The map allows **97 blocks up and 30 down** [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 色々な上限](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)), and GameWith puts the cap at 100 measured from the sea floor [unverified] ([GameWith](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545342)). Tables, counters, shelves, walls and fences have no published height in blocks, and builders simply talk of 「高さ1の家具」 (height-1 furniture) [unverified] ([エアル on X](https://x.com/heal08426/status/2031607195864080596)), so "a table's top is at +1" remains a planner assumption.

## A wall-top item keeps the house standing but adds no light, heat or water

*Brief items (2), (5) for walls, and (7) for wall-mounted items.*

### The game recognises a house by a ring at door level

ccopedia's definition of a block house is "ぐるっと一周した高さ1のブロック+それと同じ高さに設置したドア", a full ring of height-1 blocks plus a door at the same height [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f)). ぽこポケ学会's tests fill in the rest: walls 1 high are enough; wooden fences and glass windows serve as walls, and a ring made entirely of doors works; no floor is needed; a ceiling with a single cell of headroom is fine; another house directly on top leaves both recognised; walls can be shared between houses but doors cannot; a block directly in front of the door on the outside cancels the house; and 3 different furniture kinds are required only at move-in [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). English guides that demand 2-high walls or a ceiling are contradicted by these tests [unverified] ([RankedBoost](https://rankedboost.com/pokopia/guides/how-to-build-houses/); [gemukan](https://www.gemukan.com/pokoa-building-033/)). The interior can be 2×2 up to 90 tiles: ccopedia built a 9×10 interior (11×12 with walls), and 攻略大百科's own table passes 9×10 and fails 10×10 [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f); [攻略大百科](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/5901)).

Height mismatches are the usual way to break recognition. A door set at the wrong height means no house [unverified] ([Game8 JP 家判定](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768198)); one player found furniture below the door went uncounted until the floor was raised to door height [unverified] ([Yahoo!知恵袋](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q11326875771)); and corners joined only diagonally do not connect [unverified] ([しばす on X](https://x.com/ShowBuyS/status/2030845662322131082)). Once a room is recognised, though, it stays a room even if its floor is dug out [unverified] ([攻略大百科](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/5901)).

### An item on a wall does not break the enclosure

No test isolates an item on a wall, but strong indirect evidence settles it. ccopedia ran its light, warmth and humidity checks on houses carrying Monster Ball lamps, hot rocks and humidity items on their wall tops, and the houses kept working, with residents reacting to every indoor change [community-tested] ([ccopedia 明かり](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n489ac4eb3252); [ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df); [ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)). Recognition reads only the ring at door level and tolerates a block or a whole house directly above, so an item at h = +1 on a wall cannot break it, and the planner should not warn about wall-top items.

### An item on a wall adds nothing to the environment

The environment answer is tested and blunt. Light sources in or on the walls do nothing, "家を構成する壁や壁上に光源を置くのはNG。無反応", with the single exception "ドアの上だけはOK" (only above the door), and a row of wall-top Monster Ball lamps that would have made the house bright gave no reaction [community-tested] ([ccopedia 明かり](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n489ac4eb3252)). Humidity items on the wall top and water under the wall were "全てカウントされていない" (none counted), and hot rocks on the wall, magma under it and magma-rock wall blocks were all ignored, "屋外、壁、壁の上下方向は全部無意味" [community-tested] ([ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd); [ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df)). The exclusion covers the wall's own material too, since block lights or neon floor tiles (light 80) used as wall blocks, grass walls and lights buried inside walls all add nothing [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f); [ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)). ccopedia turns this into a design trick: lamps on a wall top make a house look lit while it still counts as dark for a Pokémon that wants darkness.

### Kinds, favourites and the comfort trio on wall tops remain untested

No test covers wall-top items for the move-in kinds, favourites or the comfort trio. Geometry puts a wall-top item inside the recognition box (h = +1, zero tiles out), so favourites and the trio should count if a Pokémon can walk there, but the reachability rule can exclude a 1-high wall top inside a ring with no steps up to it. ぽこポケ学会 ranks the trio below avoiding disliked surroundings, meeting the liked environment and not being hungry, but above favourites; it needs **"きゅうけい/おもちゃ/かざりつけの3つが全て認識可能範囲にある"** (rest, toy and decoration all within range), and duplicate favourites add little or nothing [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). For the move-in kinds, Game8 asks for "完成した家の中に、種類の異なる家具やかざりつけを3つ以上", three or more different furniture or decoration items inside the finished house [unverified] ([Game8 JP 家判定](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768198)), which points the planner toward counting only interior columns and leaving wall tops out.

### Wall-mounted items count by the cell they hang in

In-game text says the wall light "can be fixed to a wall", the surface light is "embeddable in ceilings and walls", and frames, a corkboard, wall flowers and a card reader hang on walls [confirmed, in-game text seen via snippets] ([Serebii wall light](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/items/walllight.shtml); [Game8 surface light](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/590124); [Serebii decoration](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/items/decoration.shtml)). An air conditioner mounted 4 above the floor was recognised as indoors but did not cool; lowered to +3, it produced "涼しくなった" [community-tested, with the message as confirmed in-game text], and ccopedia warns that air conditioners and ceiling fans are the items people most often mount too high ([ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df)). That result implies the game scores a mounted item in the interior cell in front of the wall rather than in the wall cell, though no test separates the two. Wall torches give light 40 and warmth 60 when lit and the Monster Ball wall light gives light 40, while ほしぞらのカベ, the wall towel, the grass wall decoration, wall flowers and wreaths give nothing [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f); [ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)). An item on the outside face sits in an outside cell and so scores no environment points, an inference from "屋外は完全に無関係" (outside is completely irrelevant) rather than a direct test.

Sources split on whether wall items count as furniture: Game8 recommends wall-hung decorations as one of a 2×2 house's three kinds [unverified] ([Game8 JP 家判定](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/768198)), while one blog found that rugs and wall mirrors did not count [unverified] ([goccha-journal](https://goccha-journal.com/8814)). Mounts also attach to more than blocks. Partitions go only on block side faces, with wall items stuck to a partition's back face [unverified] ([Yahoo!知恵袋](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q13328796263)), and frames go straight onto the side of a big storage box [unverified] ([GameWith](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/548273)).

## Roofs shade from any height, while hanging lights must sit at +3 or lower

*Brief items (5) for roofs, (7) for ceiling items, and (8).*

### Daylight falls straight down on floor and door tiles

Natural light ignores time of day and weather, enters only from directly above, "真上から入光する（横方向は無関係）", and scales with the unshaded share of the area, **自然光の明るさ＝90×入光している面積/すみかの範囲やブロック家の総面積** [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f)). For a block house the denominator is the full footprint including walls, and only 床 cells, the door included, can be lit. A 9×10 interior therefore scores at most 90 × 91/132 ≈ 62, short of bright (100) and just above dark (60), while a 2×3 interior (4×5 with walls) scores 31.5 with no roof at all and 18 after three roof tiles, so it is dark by default and no single 80-point lamp can make it bright [community-tested]. Exactly 60 does not register as dark: three roof tiles on the 9×10 house should give 60, but four were needed, which ccopedia traces to 32-bit floats (88/132 ≈ 0.6666666865) [community-tested], and the planner's `Math.fround` already reproduces this. Windows are irrelevant, "壁の窓も明るさや暗さには全くの無関係です", and kit curtains change nothing [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f)). That refutes The Games Wiki's claims that windows let light in and that walls must be 3 high to block it, as well as Game8's advice to add windows for brightness [unverified] ([The Games Wiki](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/brightness-system); [Game8 EN](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/585695)).

### A roof needs empty space beneath it, and height is no limit

ccopedia's roof test reads "生息地やブロック家の地面・床を高さ0として、高さ2マス以上の位置にブロックを置けば屋根として機能します" (with the habitat ground or house floor as height 0, a block at height 2 or more works as a roof), demonstrated by leaving the cell above the floor empty and setting a block above that. The same tests found that **a block placed directly on the floor is not a roof**, that a roof over the door cell counts, and that **roof height has no upper limit**, since a single block at the build ceiling still shaded the house [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f); [ccopedia くらい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4691e64139b0)). Solid stacks are a special case: a column filled with blocks up to height 4 blocks light, a 3-high stack changed nothing, and furniture piled four high (a barrel and a desk on a 2-high block) does not count as filling [community-tested] ([ccopedia くらい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4691e64139b0)).

The stack sentence ("地面や床から高さ4まで") counts four stacked blocks, so it takes the floor block as height 0. Reading the roof sentence the same way gives a rule in walking-layer terms: **a block at h ≥ +1 with empty space beneath it shades its column, as does a solid column of blocks from h = 0 to +3, while blocks standing on the floor and shorter solid stacks do not.** The two possible readings of "height 2" disagree only about the lowest possible ceiling, a single block with only the walking cell beneath it, and nobody tested that case directly. Roofs over walls or over the outer range change nothing, because walls are never lit and outside cells are not in the ratio [community-tested] ([ccopedia 明かり](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n489ac4eb3252)). Only soil and sea-rock roofs were tested, so glass, ice and fences as roof material remain unknown.

### Hanging lights count only where their light source sits

Ceiling items have their own in-game descriptions: the antique chandelier hangs "from the ceiling", the party ball is "suspended from" it, the pulley is "天井につけられる滑車" (a pulley fixed to the ceiling) and the wire is "天井から家具を吊り下げるのにピッタリ" (for hanging furniture from the ceiling) [confirmed, in-game text] ([Serebii decoration](https://www.serebii.net/pokemonpokopia/items/decoration.shtml); [GameWith 建物一覧](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/545874)). In block houses the +3 cap applies to the light source, and each hanging light puts its source at a different depth. With the ceiling at +5, the pop lamp (ポップなランプ) put its light at +3 and counted, while the mirror ball and the fluorescent light hung at +4 and did not, which ccopedia sums up as "天井からぶら下げるタイプの照明器具は、天井が思ってるより低めじゃないと明るさとしては機能しません" (hanging lights only work if the ceiling is lower than you would expect) [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f); [ccopedia 明かり](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n489ac4eb3252)).

The Small office kit shows the cost: **its 16 ceiling block lights sit 5 above the ground and give brightness 0** [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f)). Ceiling fans, each warmth −60 and working only when powered and switched on, follow the same cap [community-tested] ([ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df)), whereas kit interiors have no cap, so a single ceiling grand light makes one bright [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f)). For planning, a 1-tall hanging light needs its ceiling block at h ≤ +4, and that same block blocks daylight, so in a block house lighting from the ceiling and keeping daylight work against each other.

### In stacked houses, one storey's floor is the roof below

A house built on top of another is its own house with its own residents, and ぽこポケ学会's "most efficient" capacity design is 「積層可能」 (stackable) [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). Game8 suggests 3 blocks of headroom with the 4th serving as both ceiling and upper floor [unverified] ([Game8 EN houses](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)). In that layout the windows overlap twice, and neither overlap has been tested: the shared block sits at the lower house's +3, inside its environment band, and at the upper house's −1, inside that band too, while the upper house's walking layer sits at the lower house's +4, the disputed edge of the lower recognition box. Ver.1.1.0 added a floor choice when moving a Pokémon into a two-storey house, "室内でも引っ越し先のフロアを選べるようになった" [confirmed, official patch note] ([pocoapokemon.jp news](https://www.pocoapokemon.jp/ja/news/89/); [攻略大百科 Ver.1.1.0](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/5597)), which most likely concerns kits rather than stacked block houses.

## Habitats read light and water across ±4 but give recipe parts no height slack

*Brief items (3) for habitats, and (4).*

### A habitat's environment uses the whole box, except for warmth

Light and humidity count anywhere within ±4 vertically and 4 tiles out, with no indoor or outdoor rule; walls or floors hiding a lamp make no difference, "壁や床でランプの光を遮光するとかは全く関係がありません", and a lamp on top of a roof still counts [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f); [ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)). Warmth is the exception and uses the −1 to +3 band even in habitats [community-tested] ([ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df)). Daylight is computed over the habitat's range area [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f)), layered habitats pick up humidity from levels above or below [community-tested] ([ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd)), and the thresholds match block houses: bright at 100+, dark at 60 or less, moist at 100+, dry at 30 or less, and warm or cool at ±60 [community-tested].

### Recipe parts have no documented height tolerance

No source gives a vertical tolerance for recipe parts. Troubleshooting guides report a habitat that failed until its furniture was moved down onto the floor, and they advise checking that heights have not drifted (高さがズレていないか) and putting the parts on one plane with no gaps [unverified] ([all0kay](https://all0kay.com/poco-a-pokemon-hint-trouble/)); one player found that a block placed directly over a dwelling's grass did not break it [unverified] ([リリィ on X](https://x.com/__ririmero/status/2029761931813474412)). Habitats work inside houses and add their own residents beyond a house's four, so a 2×2 house with two small habitats holds 4 + 2 [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). Game8 adds that a block house's furniture counts for every habitat sharing the space [unverified] ([Game8 EN houses](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)), and furniture-only habitats such as the picnic set can absorb furniture so that it stops counting toward the house [unverified] ([Yahoo!知恵袋](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q11326926181)). The planner's ±4 rule is therefore right for a habitat's area and for its light and humidity, wrong for its warmth, and unsupported for deciding whether the parts form a habitat at all.

### High ground is an absolute height, about 45 above the map floor

"Elevated" recipes need the grass itself on high ground [unverified] ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/wikis/pokopia/elevated-tall-grass-habitat/)), and high ground is an absolute world height: **about 45 blocks above the map's unbreakable floor**, specifically 45 in Withered Wasteland, Bleak Beach and Palette Town, 47 in Rocky Ridges, and a separate figure for each island in Sparkling Skylands. Game8 showed this with screenshots at 44 and 45, and noted that in caves the white wind does not appear even where the height qualifies [community-tested] ([Game8 JP 高台](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769075)). Measured from the Pokémon Center, the threshold is 3 to 12 blocks depending on the town, with Game8 and 攻略大百科 one block apart in two towns [community-tested] ([攻略大百科 生息地](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4039)). A waterfall needs at least 5 blocks of drop [unverified] ([攻略大百科](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/4039)). A relative 0–9 terrain scale cannot express a 45-block threshold, so high ground needs a per-town offset rather than a local bump. A sorter's community box, finally, must sit wholly within 4 tiles across, 10 above and 8 below the habitat's dotted area [unverified] ([winter8636 on X](https://x.com/winter8636/status/2037171863265353976)), which the planner's −12 to +14 window around a home's base already matches.

## Kit interiors ignore height, and ten kits are really block houses

*Brief item (6).*

### Block-built kits are a named list, not a size tier

Kits split by what they build, not by size tier. Game8's tell is the kit picture: kits shown with a background build a block house that can then be extended with blocks [unverified] ([Game8 JP 建築のコツ](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/771730)). 攻略大百科's capacity list puts the **Pink, Orange, Gray and Yellow huts and cottages, the Log house and the Small office** in the 4-resident group, adding that block houses "どんなサイズであっても必ず4匹" (always take 4, whatever the size) [unverified, full-read guide] ([攻略大百科 家一覧](https://gamepedia.jp/pocoapokemon/archives/3442)). Game8's kit pages give capacity 4 for all of those plus the DLC Light-blue cottage (みずいろのいえ) and list each kit's bundled furniture, including a ladder and a handrail for the Yellow cottage [unverified] ([Game8 JP ログハウス](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/771906); [Game8 JP きいろいいえ](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/771922); [Game8 JP みずいろのいえ](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/805211)). Game8 EN gives block-house interiors of 4×3 for the Pink, Orange and Yellow huts, 6×3 for the Gray hut and 6×6 for the Pink cottage [unverified] ([Game8 EN houses](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)), and Bulbapedia calls the Orange hut and Log cabin "made out of blocks" and therefore impossible to relocate or demolish [unverified, snippets] ([Bulbapedia Orange hut](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Orange_hut_kit); [Bulbapedia Log cabin](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Log_cabin_kit)). ccopedia supplies the test: the log house kit builds a block house, and the Small office follows block-house height rules [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f)). The Leaf, Sand, Stone and City dens, huts, cottages and two-floor houses, the Ball house and the Tent are fixed houses with their own interiors.

### Height stops mattering inside a fixed kit

A fixed kit loads a separate map, and "屋内ならどこに置いても大丈夫です" (anywhere indoors is fine). Anything indoors counts at any height and there is no natural light; curtains, wallpaper and floors have no effect; light is bright at 40+ and dark at 40 or less, humidity moist at 40+ and dry at 60 or less, and warmth ±60 as everywhere; and blocks, water and magma cannot be placed inside [community-tested] ([ccopedia 完全版](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/ndadc82daf39f); [ccopedia うるおい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n48ddc4e1afbd); [ccopedia あたたかい](https://note.com/ccopedia/n/n4b1e798887df)). Because no block can go inside, the support-block trick cannot float anything there, so any stacking inside a kit is furniture on furniture, and nobody has documented items on shelves or tables inside kits. The furniture grids are 4×3 for a den, 6×5 for a hut, 10×8 for a cottage and 14×10 on each floor of a two-floor house [unverified, guide data] ([The Games Wiki remodel](https://thegameswiki.com/pokopia/wiki/how-to-remodel-a-house); [Game8 EN houses](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)), and the only published interior heights are 4 for the City cottage and 3 for the Poké Ball house [unverified, snippets] ([Bulbapedia City cottage](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/City_cottage_kit); [Bulbapedia Poké Ball house](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Ball_house_kit)). Wallpaper's in-game text is "大きな家で使えるカベ紙。部屋のカベや床、天井に貼れる。" (wallpaper for big houses, for a room's walls, floor and ceiling) [confirmed, in-game text] ([GameWith ポップなカベ](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/571288)), and it works only in kit houses that are not block houses [unverified] ([GameWith 模様替え](https://gamewith.jp/pocoapokemon/549794)).

### Two-floor kits split residents by floor, but scoring across floors is unknown

Two-floor kits hold 4 residents, two per floor [unverified, guide data] ([Game8 EN houses](https://game8.co/games/Pokemon-Pokopia/archives/584771)), and ぽこポケ学会 counts each two-floor りっぱな kit as 2 houses toward the 40-kit cap because entering the upper floor switches map [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会 色々な上限](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/limitation/)). Whether favourites and environment are judged per floor rests on one answer, "おそらく共通。何も置いてない２階のポケモンが住心地最高になってたんで" (probably shared, because a Pokémon on an empty second floor had top comfort) [unverified] ([Yahoo!知恵袋](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q11326372311)). A block-built kit with a loft, such as the Yellow cottage [unverified] ([Bulbapedia Yellow cottage](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/yellow_cottage_kit)), is scored by the block rules instead, so loft items count for the environment only if they sit at +3 or lower, and the 2-per-floor split does not apply.

### Move-in needs three different kinds, in kits as in block houses

Every source agrees a kit needs three pieces of furniture before a Pokémon moves in, and the Japanese sources sharpen this to **three different kinds**, "同じ家具を3個置いても条件を満たしません" (three of the same furniture does not meet it) [unverified] ([gemukan](https://www.gemukan.com/pokoa-building-001/)), matching ぽこポケ学会's "(引っ越し時のみ)3種類の異なる家具" (3 different kinds, at move-in only) for block houses [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)). Game8 says dropping below three while redecorating stops the building counting as a house [unverified] ([Game8 JP 模様替え](https://game8.jp/pocoapokemon/769142)), which conflicts with ぽこポケ学会's "move-in only". A kit house's entrance tile and the tile above it must stay clear [community-tested] ([ぽこポケ学会](https://kyu49.github.io/Pokopia/house/)), but nobody has documented whether a fixed kit has any outdoor range, so the planner's 4-tile ring around kits is a guess.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the Z layer is for. Pokopia scores a voxel grid through masks anchored at the door's level and never consults support, so one integer level per object plus an attachment type (standing, wall face or ceiling) is enough to reproduce every tested count, and support relations matter only to the editor, to block impossible placements and to show which items float. The real modelling risks are not geometric: reachability can silently drop items the masks include, two one-level edges (furniture at +4 and the lowest possible ceiling) are disputed, and stacked houses create overlapping masks nobody has measured, so the planner should report three states, "counts", "probably counts" and "does not count", rather than a yes or no. Building roofs from real blocks also exposes a trade-off the quarter slider hides: the block that makes a house dark is the same block a hanging lamp hangs from, and it must sit at +4 or lower for that lamp to count.

### Rules the Z layer should follow

| # | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store an integer z on every block, liquid, rug and object, and let one (x, y) hold several things at different z. Terrain elevation becomes the z of the lowest standing layer. No half-levels outdoors; kit interiors keep half-tile x/y steps instead. | Whole-block heights [community-tested]; ±2 placement step [unverified] |
| 2 | For each home compute h = z − z_door, where z_door is the door's walking layer. The floor block is h = −1, and a tile's surface block sits one level below whatever stands on it. Score every item by its own cell, never by what it rests on. | ccopedia convention [community-tested] |
| 3 | Detect a block house on the z_door layer only: a ring of blocks, fences, glass windows or doors at z_door, joined edge to edge, with a door at the same level, an interior of 2×2 to 90 tiles, and no block at z_door in front of the door. Ignore everything at z_door + 1 and above (wall-top items, taller walls, ceilings, another house). No floor is required. | [community-tested]; diagonal joins [unverified] |
| 4 | Recognition box: x/y within 4 tiles of the house's outer walls or the habitat footprint; h from −4 to +3 counts, and h = +4 shows as "probably counts". Flag items with no walkable path (wall tops inside a closed ring, floating items) as "counts only if reachable". | [community-tested]; +4 disputed |
| 5 | Move-in check: count distinct item kinds in interior columns at h −1 to +3, including decorations hung on the inside face of walls; leave out wall tops and outside items. Apply the same kinds rule to kits instead of a piece count. | Kinds [community-tested]; where they must stand [unverified] |
| 6 | Block-house environment: interior and door columns only, h −1 to +3. Floor material counts at h = −1; a tile dug one deep counts its contents but not its floor. Wall columns never count at any h, light-giving wall blocks included. Inside-face wall mounts score in the cell in front of the wall. | [community-tested]; mount cell inferred |
| 7 | Habitat environment: light and humidity anywhere in the ±4 box with nothing blocking; warmth only at h −1 to +3. Recipe parts belong on one surface level, so warn, but do not fail, when a part sits higher than the rest. | [community-tested]; recipe parts [unverified] |
| 8 | Roof: a floor or door column is roofed when a block sits over it at h ≥ +1 with empty space beneath, at any height up to the build ceiling, or when solid blocks fill h 0 to +3. Furniture never roofs. Daylight = 90 × fround(open floor and door columns ÷ all house tiles including walls); ignore windows, time and weather. An upper storey's floor roofs the house below. | [community-tested]; the h = +1 case is untested |
| 9 | Ceiling items attach to the underside of a block at z_c and hang into z_c − 1, or two cells for the pop lamp. In block houses they score only if their light-source cell is at h ≤ +3, so a 1-tall hanging light needs z_c ≤ z_door + 4. Wall items attach to (x, y, z, face). | [community-tested] |
| 10 | Never drop or delete items when the block below them goes; mark them as floating. At placement, require a block below for rugs, grass, flowers and the wooden-fence door, and a block above for the mirror ball and other ceiling items; refuse furniture on floating weed. Keep an `acceptsOnTop` flag: true for tables, large tables, stands, the washing machine, big storage boxes, the wireless transmitter, blocks and pillars; false for floating weed; unknown for everything else. | Mixed [community-tested] and [unverified] |
| 11 | Treat every ring and door at its own z_door as a separate house with its own 4 residents and 3 kinds, and mark items and blocks that fall in two houses' masks as "overlap, untested". | Stacking [community-tested]; overlap untested |
| 12 | Kit interiors: no blocks, water or magma, and the environment counts at any height. Two-floor fixed kits give 2 resident slots per floor, with scoring across floors flagged as unverified. Block-built kits (the Pink, Orange, Gray and Yellow huts and cottages, the Log cabin, the Aqua cottage and the Small office) follow the block rules and take 4 residents however many floors they have. | [community-tested] and [unverified] |
| 13 | Cap z at 97 above and 30 below the map's reference level, and store a per-town absolute offset so that high ground (about 45 above the unbreakable floor) can be judged. | [community-tested] |
